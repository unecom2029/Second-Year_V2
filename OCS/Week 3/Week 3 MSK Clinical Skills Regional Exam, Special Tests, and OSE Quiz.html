Week 3 MSK - High-Yield One-Pager

Condensed by learning objective: static screen/gait - template rules - motion/strength - regional OSE - special tests - case reasoning.

LO 1 - Static Screen & Gait

Static Screening Exam

  • Introduce, ID patient, sanitize hands, consent, drape/expose region, plain language.
  • Patient standing, minimal clothing; observe posterior -> lateral -> anterior.
  • Posterior: mastoid tilt, shoulder height, scapular position, spinous line, iliac crest height, PSIS, gluteal folds, popliteal creases, malleoli.
  • Lateral: EAM-shoulder-greater trochanter-knee-lateral malleolus plumb line; check cervical/thoracic/lumbar curve depth.
  • Anterior: head position, clavicle/AC symmetry, ASIS height, patellar position, foot arches.
  • Any asymmetry/lean/curve exaggeration flags a region for focused regional exam.

Gait Analysis

  • Stance (~60% cycle): heel strike -> foot flat -> midstance -> heel-off -> toe-off.
  • Swing (~40% cycle): acceleration -> midswing -> deceleration.
  • Watch stride length/symmetry, cadence, base of support, arm swing, pelvic rotation, trunk lean.
  • Heel walk screens L4/L5 (tibialis anterior/dorsiflexors); toe walk screens S1 (gastroc/soleus/plantarflexors).
  • Tandem (heel-to-toe) walk screens balance/cerebellar function.
  • Antalgic gait = shortened stance on painful side; Trendelenburg gait = pelvic drop or trunk lean over stance leg from weak ipsilateral gluteus medius; steppage/circumduction suggests foot drop.

Regional Testing Template

  • Test symptomatic joint plus joint above and joint below; always compare bilaterally.
  • Inspection: skin (color/swelling/scars), muscle bulk/tone, side-to-side symmetry.
  • Palpation for TART: Tissue texture change, Asymmetry, Restriction of motion, Tenderness.
  • AROM = patient actively moves (physiologic motion; assesses willingness/function/pain).
  • PROM = examiner passively moves joint (anatomic motion; assesses end-feel, isolates joint vs. muscle limit).
  • Strength testing = resisted manual testing through each plane, graded 0-5/5, performed after AROM/PROM.
  • Sequence: inspect -> palpate TART -> AROM -> PROM -> strength -> special tests as indicated.

History & Red Flags

  • Onset, mechanism of injury, location/radiation, dominant side, timing/duration, age, gender, PMH/prior injury.
  • Red flags: fever + hot swollen joint (septic joint); weight loss/night pain/cancer history (malignancy); bowel/bladder dysfunction or saddle anesthesia (cauda equina).
  • Suspected fracture/major trauma -> avoid stressing limb; check distal pulses, sensation, cap refill first.
  • No consent, or patient reports pain/asks to stop -> stop or modify exam immediately.
LO 2-3 - Motion, Strength & Grading

Strength Grading (0-5/5)

  • 0/5: no palpable or visible contraction.
  • 1/5 (trace): visible/palpable twitch, no joint motion produced.
  • 2/5 (poor): full ROM only with gravity eliminated (limb supported in horizontal plane).
  • 3/5 (fair): full ROM against gravity, no added manual resistance.
  • 4/5 (good): full ROM against gravity + some resistance, less than normal.
  • 5/5 (normal): full ROM against gravity and full manual resistance.
  • Grade each motion bilaterally, compare sides, document grade, explain findings without jargon.

Motions Tested by Region

  • Spine/trunk: flexion, extension, side-bending (L/R), rotation (L/R).
  • Shoulder/hip: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, internal rotation, external rotation.
  • Forearm: pronation, supination.
  • Wrist/hand: flexion, extension, radial/ulnar deviation, opposition (thumb-to-digit), grip.
  • Elbow/knee: flexion, extension; knee also tested with valgus/varus stress.
  • Ankle/foot: dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion.
  • Order: AROM first (active), then PROM (passive; note end-feel: soft/firm/hard/empty), then resisted strength each plane.

Cervical Spine

  • AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension, side-bending, rotation L/R; palpate OA, AA, C2-C7 facets, paraspinals for TART.
  • Spurling's test: extend + side-bend/rotate head toward symptomatic side + axial compression -> reproduces radicular arm pain = positive, cervical nerve root (foraminal) compression.
  • Cervical distraction test: manual traction relieves radicular arm symptoms = positive, supports nerve root compression/foraminal stenosis (opposite mechanism of Spurling's).
  • Vertebral artery test: passive extension + rotation held ~30s, watch for nystagmus/dizziness/dysarthria = positive, suggests vertebrobasilar insufficiency - stop exam, avoid HVLA.
  • Hoffman's/Tromner's sign, hyperreflexia, clonus, Lhermitte's sign (flexion -> electric shock down spine) screen for cervical myelopathy/cord compression.

Thoracic & Lumbar Spine

  • AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension, side-bending, rotation; assess rib cage excursion on inhale/exhale in thoracic region.
  • Palpate spinous/transverse processes, paraspinals, SI joints, PSIS for TART.
  • Straight leg raise (SLR): passive hip flexion, knee extended, 30-70 degrees reproduces radicular leg pain = positive, L4-S1 nerve root tension/disc herniation; crossed SLR is more specific.
  • Slump test: seated slump + neck flexion + knee extension + ankle dorsiflexion reproduces radicular pain = positive, lumbosacral nerve root tension.
  • FABER/Patrick's test: Flexion-ABduction-External Rotation (figure-4); posterior/gluteal pain = SI joint, groin pain = hip joint (OA/labrum).
LO 4-5 - UE & LE Regional Testing

Shoulder

  • AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, IR, ER; note painful arc ~60-120 degrees abduction.
  • Neer's test: passive forward flexion, scapula stabilized -> pain = positive, subacromial impingement.
  • Hawkins-Kennedy: shoulder flexed 90 + forced IR -> pain = positive, subacromial impingement.
  • Empty can/Jobe's test: resisted abduction at 90 degrees, thumbs down -> weakness/pain = positive, supraspinatus tendinopathy/tear.
  • Drop arm test: cannot slowly lower arm from abduction (drops or severe pain) = positive, full-thickness rotator cuff tear.
  • Apprehension/relocation test: passive ER with arm abducted 90 -> apprehension = anterior instability; relief with posterior humeral pressure confirms.
  • Cross-body adduction test: AC joint pain = positive, AC joint pathology/arthritis.
  • Speed's test: resisted forward flexion, elbow extended, forearm supinated -> anterior pain = biceps tendinopathy/SLAP lesion.
  • Yergason's test: resisted supination with elbow flexed 90 -> bicipital groove pain = biceps tendon pathology.

Elbow & Wrist/Hand

  • Elbow AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension, pronation, supination.
  • Varus/valgus stress test at 20-30 degrees flexion: laxity/pain = LCL (varus) or MCL/UCL (valgus) instability.
  • Tinel's sign at elbow (tap cubital tunnel/medial epicondyle) -> tingling into 4th/5th digits = positive, ulnar nerve entrapment.
  • Wrist/hand AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension, radial/ulnar deviation, opposition, grip.
  • Tinel's sign at wrist and Phalen's test (wrists flexed x60s) -> paresthesias in median nerve distribution (thumb-index-middle-half ring finger) = positive, carpal tunnel syndrome.
  • Finkelstein test: thumb in fist, passive ulnar wrist deviation -> radial wrist pain = positive, De Quervain's tenosynovitis.

Hip

  • AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, IR, ER.
  • FABER/Patrick's test: groin pain = intra-articular hip pathology; posterior pain = SI joint.
  • FADIR test (Flexion-ADduction-Internal Rotation): anterior/groin pain or click = positive, femoroacetabular impingement or labral tear.
  • Trendelenburg test: single-leg stance, contralateral pelvis drop/compensatory trunk lean = positive, ipsilateral gluteus medius/minimus weakness.
  • Ober's test: side-lying, hip abducted/extended then adducted -- limb stays elevated = positive, IT band tightness/contracture.

Knee

  • AROM/PROM/strength: flexion, extension; note effusion, quadriceps bulk/tone.
  • Anterior drawer test: knee flexed 90, tibia pulled anteriorly -> excess translation = positive, ACL tear (less sensitive than Lachman).
  • Lachman test: knee flexed ~20-30, tibia pulled anteriorly on stabilized femur -> increased translation/soft end-point = positive, most sensitive test for ACL tear.
  • Posterior drawer test: tibia pushed posteriorly, knee flexed 90 -> excess posterior translation = positive, PCL tear.
  • Valgus stress (30 degrees flexion): medial joint gapping/pain = MCL injury. Varus stress: lateral joint gapping/pain = LCL injury.
  • McMurray test: tibia rotated (IR/ER) while extending flexed knee -> palpable/audible click + pain = positive, meniscal tear.
  • Patellar grind (Clarke's) test: downward patellar pressure + quad contraction -> pain = patellofemoral syndrome; patellar apprehension (lateral glide) = instability/prior dislocation.

Ankle & Foot

  • AROM/PROM/strength: dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion.
  • Anterior drawer test (ankle): stabilize tibia, pull calcaneus anteriorly -> excess translation/clunk = positive, ATFL tear.
  • Talar tilt test: inversion stress -> excess laxity/pain = positive, calcaneofibular ligament injury; eversion stress screens deltoid ligament.
  • Thompson test: squeeze calf prone with foot off table -> no plantarflexion = positive, complete Achilles tendon rupture.
  • Tinel's sign at tarsal tunnel (tap posterior to medial malleolus) -> tingling into plantar foot = positive, tarsal tunnel syndrome.
