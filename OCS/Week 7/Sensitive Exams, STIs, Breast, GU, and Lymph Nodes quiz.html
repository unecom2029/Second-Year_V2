HIGH-YIELD ONE-PAGER

Sensitive Exams — LO-Based Rapid Review

Condensed strictly by Learning Objective (LO1–LO14). Transcript-only additions are integrated as course pearls.

LO 1 · Foundation — comprehensive breast/genital/prostate/rectal exam

  • Sequence: history (why you're examining) → indicated inspection/palpation → interpret in context.
  • History: breast — lump, pain, discharge, cyclicity, FHx. Genital/pelvic — discharge, sores/lesions, bleeding, pain, urinary sx, STI risk, menstrual/reproductive hx.
  • Inspect: skin, contour, symmetry, lesions, retraction, discharge, swelling, bulges. Palpate: location, size, consistency, mobility/fixation, tenderness, warmth, nodularity, regional nodes.
  • Interpret: longstanding + unchanged → likely normal variant. New, fixed, hard, painful, inflamed, ulcerated, bleeding, rapidly enlarging, or with systemic sx → pathology, work it up.
  • Trauma-informed: ask before uncovering whether anything makes the exam difficult; narrate before touch; patient controls the drape; re-establish contact (don't surprise) if you break it.

LO 2 · USPSTF Grade A/B — who's due for screening

  • Breast: 40–74 biennial mammogram (B); ≥75 insufficient evidence (I). Average-risk only — doesn't cover high-risk genetic syndromes/prior chest radiation.
  • Cervical: 21–29 cytology q3y (A); 30–65 cytology q3y OR hrHPV q5y OR cotest q5y (A). Don't screen <21, >65 with adequate prior screening, or post-hysterectomy (D).
  • Prostate: 55–69 shared decision-making (C); ≥70 don't screen (D). Risk: older age, Black race, family history.
  • STI: chlamydia/GC — sexually active women ≤24, or older with ↑risk, or pregnant (B). HIV 15–65 (younger/older if ↑risk), all pregnant (A). Syphilis/Hep B — ↑risk + pregnant (A/B). Hep C — adults 18–79 (B).
  • Mnemonic: "40/2, 21/3, 55/choice." Key distinction: screening = asymptomatic; any new mass/spontaneous discharge/symptom moves the patient into a diagnostic pathway regardless of screening schedule. High-risk breast: start screening 10 yr before the age a first-degree relative was diagnosed.

LO 3 · When each sensitive exam is indicated

  • Screening mammogram: asymptomatic, in the age/risk group. A new mass, spontaneous unilateral discharge, or persistent skin/nipple change = diagnostic, not routine.
  • Cervical cytology/HPV: routine interval in average-risk; bleeding/discharge/lesions/pelvic pain need clinical evaluation even if screening is current.
  • Pelvic/bimanual: discharge, tenderness, STI concern, abnormal bleeding, pelvic pain, dysmenorrhea, prolapse/fullness, reproductive/contraceptive concerns, postmenopausal bleeding.
  • Hernia check: groin bulge/lump, often with cough/strain, potentially reducible. Rectal exam: bleeding, pain, itching, fissure/hemorrhoid concern, mass — not a routine "annual" maneuver without indication.
  • Prostate/PSA: shared decision for screening; diagnostic exam for urinary sx, abnormal DRE, hematuria, pelvic/back pain, weight loss, fever/dysuria.

LO 4 · Breast lumps, discomfort & discharge — normal vs. abnormal, work-up

  • Pathologic discharge (SBU+40+Mass): Spontaneous, Bloody/serous, Unilateral/uni-ductal, age ≥40, or associated mass.
  • Physiologic discharge: bilateral, multi-ductal, provoked by manipulation; also consider trauma, amenorrhea/hypogonadism (hyperprolactinemia), prolactin-raising meds.
  • History: mass (location, onset, change, cyclicity, pain, trauma, prior biopsy); discharge (appearance, spontaneous vs. provoked, uni/bilateral, uni/multiductal); risk (age, FHx/BRCA, reproductive/hormone hx, alcohol, prior atypia).
  • Exam: inspect (asymmetry, contour, skin dimpling/edema/erythema/ulceration, nipple inversion/deviation/crusting); palpate clavicle→lower rib cage, sternum→midaxillary line; nodes (cervical/supraclavicular, infraclavicular, axillary).
  • Work-up: new/concerning finding → diagnostic mammogram + targeted US → suspicious (BI-RADS 4/spiculated) → image-guided core biopsy.
  • Pearls: longstanding nipple inversion may be normal — ask if it's new; persistent itchy/flaking areolar dermatitis → think Paget disease; peau d'orange → inflammatory breast cancer (vs. mastitis/cellulitis).

LO 5 · Breast mass comparison

  • Fibroadenoma: age 15–25 (up to 55); round/disc/lobular, 1–2 cm; firm; well-delineated; very mobile ("rolls" under fingers); usually nontender.
  • Cyst: age 30–50, regresses after menopause unless on estrogen; round; soft-firm/elastic; well-delineated; mobile; often tender.
  • Cancer: age 30–90, most common >50; irregular/stellate; firm/hard; poorly delineated; may be fixed; usually nontender. Most concerning pattern: doesn't move, doesn't hurt, especially if irregular/hard.
  • Fibrocystic change: nodular, rope-like densities, age 25–50, may be tender/cyclic (worse mid-cycle), often bilateral/diffuse and symmetric — benign, not a cancer risk factor.

LO 6 · Vaginitis, PID, penile discharge & STIs

  • Trichomonas: pruritic, purulent, dyspareunia, postcoital bleeding, strawberry cervix; gray/yellow/green frothy/foamy, malodorous, +whiff; motile flagellated protozoa, pH >4.5. Tx: metronidazole.
  • Bacterial vaginosis: fishy odor (worse after sex), gray/white thin discharge, normal-looking mucosa, +whiff; clue cells, ↓lactobacilli, pH >4.5. Tx: metronidazole/clindamycin.
  • Candida: pruritus/burning, red edematous + satellite lesions, white curdy discharge; budding yeast/pseudohyphae, pH normal 3.8–4.5. Tx: azole.
  • Atrophic: itching/burning/dyspareunia/postcoital spotting/dysuria; dry pale thin mucosa (postmenopausal "tacky" feel); pH >4.5. Tx: vaginal estrogen. Mnemonic: Fish/Flagella/Fungi/Fading estrogen.
  • PID/cervicitis clue: cervical motion tenderness is never normal — pain with cervical motion raises PID/cervicitis concern; treat identified GC/CT (doxycycline + ceftriaxone) and evaluate/treat partners.
  • Chlamydia/GC: ± discharge, pelvic pain, CMT, fever; red cervix/penis with yellow-green discharge. Tx: doxycycline (chlamydia); ceftriaxone (GC).
  • Herpes: painful, grouped vesicles/ulcers on an erythematous base after a tingling/burning prodrome; HSV PCR is the test of choice. Tx: acyclovir/valacyclovir/famciclovir.
  • Painful ulcers = HSV, chancroid. Painless ulcers = syphilis (indurated chancre), LGV, granuloma inguinale. Mnemonic: "Herpes Hurts; Syphilis Silent."
  • No test-of-cure needed for cervical GC/CT treated with CDC regimens (pharyngeal GC is the exception); retest chlamydia ~3 months later.

LO 7 · Hernias — direct, indirect, femoral, umbilical, scrotal

  • Indirect: most common overall (all ages/sexes, including women); originates above the inguinal ligament near the internal ring; often descends into the scrotum ("scrotal hernia" = a descended indirect hernia, not a separate category); cough impulse hits the fingertip. "Indirect = Into."
  • Direct: less common, typically men >40; above the inguinal ligament near the pubic tubercle/external ring; rarely enters the scrotum; cough impulse pushes the side of the finger. "Direct = side Door."
  • Femoral: least common overall, but relatively more common in women; below the inguinal ligament, more lateral, may mimic a lymph node; canal is empty on cough impulse. "Femoral = Floor."
  • Umbilical: congenital defect at the umbilical ring, common in infants (usually closes by ~age 2); in adults, associated with obesity, pregnancy/multiparity, or ascites/↑intra-abdominal pressure — midline bulge at the umbilicus, typically reducible.

LO 8 · Testis, epididymis & spermatic cord

  • Varicocele: dull ache, better lying down, usually left-sided; soft "bag of worms," collapses supine, accentuated by Valsalva; may affect fertility. W/u: CPE, semen analysis, US, FSH/LH. Tx: surgery.
  • Torsion: acute severe pain, swollen/tender testis, often retracted upward, red/edematous, ± abdominal pain/N/V/fever; peak age 12–18. UA should be negative; US. Surgical emergency — manual detorsion may be attempted but does not replace surgery.
  • Epididymitis: acutely inflamed, swollen, tender epididymis; scrotum red/tender/edematous; causes: GC, CT, E. coli, Pseudomonas, trauma, autoimmune. W/u: UA/culture, STI swab, US. Tx: ceftriaxone + doxycycline (or levofloxacin).
  • Testicular tumor: hard, painless nodule, increasing heaviness; age 20–35; risks: cryptorchidism, FHx. W/u: imaging/markers/CT. Tx: orchiectomy ± node resection/chemo.
  • Mnemonic: "Worms/Twist/Tender tube/Tumor." Torsion vs. epididymitis: sudden onset + high/retracted testis → torsion; slower onset → epididymitis.

LO 9 · Prostate exam — normal, BPH, prostatitis, cancer

  • DRE: standing bent over table (or left lateral decubitus); narrate throughout; lubricate; have the patient bear down to relax the sphincter; sweep the rectal wall, palpate prostate at 6 o'clock.
  • Normal: no sx, normal frequency/stream; rounded, heart-shaped, ~2.5 cm, palpable median sulcus; PSA 0–2.5.
  • Prostatitis: fever + UTI-type sx (frequency, urgency, dysuria, incomplete voiding, low-back/pelvic pain); enlarged, tender, "boggy," warm, painful DRE. PSA may rise then normalize. Tx: 4–6 wk antibiotics.
  • BPH: urgency, frequency, nocturia, ↓stream, incomplete emptying, straining; symmetrically enlarged, smooth, firm; median sulcus may be reduced. PSA may be elevated. Tx: tamsulosin, finasteride, surgery.
  • Cancer: may mimic BPH ± low-back/pelvic pain, hematuria, weight loss; hard/firm, possibly irregular nodules. PSA often elevated but can be <4. Referral for imaging/biopsy; tx hormone/radiation/prostatectomy.
  • Mnemonic: "Boggy hurts, Big smooth, Bumpy hard." Referral trigger: PSA >4 → repeat to confirm persistence → if still high, risk assessment + multiparametric MRI + urology referral/biopsy.

LO 10 · Incontinence — stress, urge, overflow, mixed

  • Stress: small leaks with cough/sneeze/laugh/lifting, no preceding urge; weak sphincter/pelvic floor (childbirth, surgery, obesity, prostate surgery). Tx: Kegels, weight loss, pessary, surgery.
  • Urge: sudden, strong, involuntary urge, not activity-related; overactive detrusor (infection/tumor/fecal impaction can contribute). Tx: Kegels, bladder training, anticholinergics/beta-agonists.
  • Overflow: chronic retention → dribbling, weak stream; weak detrusor or outlet obstruction (BPH/tumor, S2–S4 nerve disease, diabetic neuropathy). Tx: catheter as needed, timed voiding, treat underlying cause.
  • Mixed: combination of stress + urge (± overflow); treat each component.
  • Mnemonic: "Squeeze/Suddenly/Spill-over." Initial eval: screening questionnaire, meds, alcohol/caffeine, QoL impact, voiding diary; UA ± culture if UTI/hematuria suspected. Detailed neuro exam only if sudden-onset or new neuro symptoms.

LO 11 · Comprehensive exam — practical technique

  • Breast: seated inspection (arms side → overhead → hips/back → lean forward) → supine palpation → supraclavicular + infraclavicular + axillary nodes. "Supermarket aisles" grid, 3 flat fingers, superficial→medium→deep spiral. Cover clavicle to 2–3 fingerbreadths below breast tissue, sternum to midaxillary line.
  • Female: external genital exam starts at the thigh; patient controls the drape; test speculum light/inspect for cracks before insertion.
  • Male: scrotal/penile sequence — inspect skin → pulses/nodes → hernia check → scrotal contents → shaft/glans/meatus.
  • Universal: communicate before every step, maintain continuous predictable contact when the patient can't see your hands, and re-establish contact if you break it.

LO 12 · Axillary + inguinal lymphatic H&P

  • Axillary nodes: drain arm, breast, thorax — check with breast mass/discharge, upper-extremity infection/inflammation, local skin lesions.
  • Inguinal nodes: palpated with femoral pulses during the external genital/groin exam — relevant for genital lesions/STIs, lower-extremity or groin disease.
  • Supraclavicular: part of the regional breast exam, high-yield when enlarged. Right side drains mediastinum/lungs; left side (Virchow node) drains the abdomen.

LO 13 · Normal vs. pathologic lymph nodes

  • Size: >1 cm in an adult merits attention (children can normally have larger reactive nodes).
  • Consistency: hard → cancer or inflammation-induced fibrosis; firm/rubbery → lymphoma, chronic leukemia; soft → acute leukemia.
  • Fixation: fixed/matted → cancer invading local tissue. Tenderness: favors inflammation, but rapid enlargement, hemorrhage, immune stimulation, and some malignancies can also be tender — reassuring only in context.
  • Duration: work up if present >4 weeks, or sooner with concerning symptoms/risk factors (age >40, male sex, white race, supraclavicular location, systemic symptoms: fever, chills, fatigue, weight loss, arthralgias, rash, weakness).
  • Mnemonic: "HARD sticks, RUBBER bounces, TENDER inflames."

LO 14 · Lymphadenopathy — clinical implications

  • Localized: follow the regional drainage territory — more likely infectious/inflammatory.
  • Generalized: enlargement of ≥2 non-contiguous node groups — think systemic infection, autoimmune disease, or disseminated malignancy.
  • Reasoning sequence: localized or generalized? → which drainage territory? → node texture/fixation/tenderness → duration + fever/chills/weight loss/fatigue → observe vs. accelerate work-up.
  • Red flag: enlarged left supraclavicular node = Virchow node; when associated with malignancy, the board term is Troisier sign. Generalized nodes + systemic symptoms should move faster than an isolated, reassuring node.
