Week 5 Cardiovascular

High-Yield LO One-Pager

LO1 · Blood Pressure — measure, apply, interpret

  • Prep: quiet room, no caffeine/nicotine/exercise ×30 min, empty bladder, rest 3–5 min without talking.
  • Position: back/feet supported, legs uncrossed, bare arm supported at heart level, correct cuff size 2–3 cm above the antecubital crease.
  • Read: SBP = sound at ≥2 consecutive beats; DBP = sounds muffle/disappear.
  • Repeat ≥2× ≥30 sec apart, average.
  • Error → shift: cuff over clothing 5–50 mmHg; full bladder 10; talking 10; unsupported arm 10; unsupported back/feet 6; crossed legs 2–8; wrong cuff size 2–10.
  • Apply/interpret: before treating a number as real, confirm technique was valid and use the averaged, repeated reading — this lecture gives technique, not HTN staging cutoffs.

LO2 · Orthostatic Vitals — measure, apply, interpret

  • How: supine BP/HR after rest, then repeat within 3 min of standing.
  • Positive (20/10/3): SBP ↓≥20 or DBP ↓≥10 mmHg within 3 min standing; symptoms (dizziness, weakness, visual change, near-syncope) support it.
  • Expected normal response: HR ↑~11, SBP ↓~3.5, DBP ~↑5.2.
  • Interpret the HR response: no compensatory HR rise → think autonomic dysfunction; HR rise ≥30 bpm ± supine tachycardia → clue for hypovolemia.
  • Applied: 126/76 HR 70 supine → 102/66 HR 92 standing = SBP −24/DBP −10 (positive) with an appropriate HR rise → favors volume-mediated over autonomic cause.

LO3 · JVD & HJR — evaluate and interpret

  • JVP setup: HOB 30–45°, tangential light; jugular pulsation is non-palpable, biphasic, varies with respiration/position (vs. carotid: palpable, doesn't vary).
  • Measure/convert: vertical height above sternal angle; cm H₂O ÷1.36 = mmHg (11 cm ≈ 8 mmHg); JVP visible while seated ≈10 cm.
  • Interpret elevated JVP: ↑ right atrial/central venous pressure — right HF, TR, constrictive/restrictive physiology, tamponade, SVC/IVC obstruction.
  • HJR technique: firm 20–35 mmHg mid-abdominal pressure ×15–30 sec; patient breathes normally, doesn't strain.
  • Interpret: normal = no rise, or rise <4 cm and/or resolves <10 sec. Positive = sustained rise ≥4 cm for ≥10 sec → right HF/TR, constrictive/tamponade physiology, IVC obstruction; can predict PCWP ≥15 mmHg.

LO4 · Auscultation — posts, technique, documentation

  • 4 posts + Erb's: Aortic 2nd ICS RSB · Pulmonic 2nd ICS LSB · Erb's point (between pulmonic/tricuspid) · Tricuspid 5th ICS LLSB · Mitral/apex 5th ICS MCL.
  • Use diaphragm (high-pitched: S1, S2, most murmurs) and bell (low-pitched: S3, S4, MS rumble) at every post.
  • Sequence: state rate + rhythm → identify S1/S2 → screen for extra sounds/murmurs/gallops → if present, fully characterize (SCRIPT below) → link finding to a disease process.
  • SCRIPT: Site, Configuration, Radiation, Intensity (grade), Pitch/quality, Timing.
  • Document — normal: "Rate 72, regular rhythm. S1/S2 normal. No murmurs or gallops." Abnormal: "Regular rate/rhythm. Grade 3/6 mid-systolic crescendo–decrescendo murmur, maximal at the aortic area, radiates to the neck, no thrill."

LO5 · Heart Sounds — S1, S2, splitting, S3, S4

  • S1 "Lub": mitral+tricuspid closure, onset of systole; loudest apex, diaphragm.
  • S2 "Dub": aortic+pulmonic closure, end systole/onset diastole; loudest base, diaphragm.
  • Physiologic split: widens with inspiration (↓intrathoracic pressure → ↑RV filling → delays P2), narrows/fuses with expiration.
  • Fixed split: present equally in inspiration and expiration → classic for ASD.
  • Paradoxical split (as taught): reversed pattern, widens on expiration → RBBB, pulmonary hypertension.
  • S3 "Kentucky": early diastole, rapid filling into a compliant/volume-loaded ventricle; apex, LLD, bell. Can be normal (young/athlete/pregnancy) or pathologic (HF, valve disease, dilated cardiomyopathy).
  • S4 "Tennessee": late diastole, atrial kick into a stiff LV (LVH/CAD); apex, LLD, bell. Always abnormal; absent in AF (needs organized atrial contraction).

LO6 · Systolic vs. Diastolic Murmurs — compare and grade

  • Systolic: holosystolic = constant gradient → MR/TR. Mid-systolic crescendo–decrescendo = ejection → AS. Early systolic (starts with S1) → acute MR/TR w/o pulm HTN. Late systolic, high-pitched → MVP ± click, ischemic MR.
  • Diastolic: early, decrescendo, starts at S2 → AR. Mid-diastolic (↑flow/obstruction across AV valves) → MS/MR/TR/TS/ASD. Presystolic (coincides with atrial kick) → MS/TS or atrial myxoma.
  • Systolic grade 1–6: 1 barely audible → 2 slight → 3 moderate, no thrill → 4 loud + palpable thrill → 5 very loud, thrill easily felt → 6 audible off the chest.
  • Diastolic grade 1–4: 1 very soft → 2 soft → 3 moderate → 4 loud/thrill.

LO7 · Clubbing, Cyanosis, Edema — findings + causes

  • Clubbing: ↑nail-fold (Lovibond) angle, bulbous distal digit, loss of Schamroth's window. Causes: pulmonary (bronchial CA, fibrosing alveolitis, chronic suppuration/bronchiectasis/CF/empyema, mesothelioma); cardiac (cyanotic CHD, endocarditis, atrial myxoma); GI/other (cirrhosis, IBD, celiac, GI lymphoma, thyroid acropachy).
  • Cyanosis — peripheral = COLD: Cold exposure, Obstruction (arterial/venous), LV failure/shock, Decreased cardiac output. Central = PALMS: Polycythemia, Altitude, Lung disease, Methemoglobinemia, Shunt (R→L).
  • Edema: grade 0–4+ by pitting depth/recovery time. HF edema is typically bilateral, dependent, pitting — but nonspecific; also seen with venous insufficiency, lymphedema, May–Thurner, hypoalbuminemia, and medications.

LO8 · Lower-Extremity Ulcers — arterial vs. venous vs. neurotrophic vs. traumatic vs. malignant

  • Arterial: pressure points/toes/heel; small, deep, "punched-out," regular border; pale/dry base, little drainage; painful; cool skin, cap refill >3s, weak/absent pulses; minimal edema.
  • Venous: medial gaiter/malleolus; large, shallow, irregular border; moist/granulating, heavy drainage; typically painless; warm, stasis dermatitis/hyperpigmentation, intact pulses; edema + varicosities present.
  • Neurotrophic: plantar/pressure-bearing points in a neuropathic foot; deep with surrounding callus; little pain (impaired sensation) — key clue is neuropathy + pressure point + callus.
  • Traumatic: shape/location follows the injury mechanism (pressure, friction, laceration, burn); variable pain/depth; history of a matching local injury.
  • Malignant: chronic, nonhealing, or changing ulcer with everted/rolled or irregular edges, induration, friability, or bleeding → biopsy for tissue diagnosis.

LO9 · History/Exam Features → Ischemia, ACS, AMI, CHF

  • ↑ suspicion for ischemia/ACS/AMI: central/retrosternal pressure, squeezing, heaviness, exertional trigger. LR+: right-arm radiation 4.7, both-arm radiation 4.1, exertional 2.4, left-arm radiation 2.3, diaphoresis 2.0, nausea/vomiting 1.9, worse-than-prior-angina/like-prior-MI 1.8, described as pressure 1.3. Larger pain area → 2.2× higher odds of positive troponin.
  • ↓ suspicion: sharp/fleeting/shifting/pleuritic/positional pain. Levine sign/palm/arm-pointing gestures have low sensitivity — don't overweight them.
  • CHF history (LR+): PND ~2.6, orthopnea ~2.2, edema ~2.1 (nonspecific), DOE ~1.3; bendopnea flags advanced HF/low cardiac index.
  • CHF exam (LR+): S3 ~11.4 (strongest, spec ~0.99), displaced apical impulse lateral to MCL (10.3 for low EF, 13.0 for CAD, 5.8 for ↑filling pressure), HJR/AJR ~6.4, JVD ~5.1 (JVP ≥12 mmHg = highest PPV), crackles ~2.8, any murmur ~2.6, LE edema ~2.3 (nonspecific).
  • Integrate the full pattern (history + exam) rather than any single finding or gesture when weighing ischemia/ACS/AMI vs. CHF.
