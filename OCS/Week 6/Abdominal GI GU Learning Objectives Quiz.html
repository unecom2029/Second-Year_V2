ABDOMINAL GI/GU · HIGH-YIELD OBJECTIVE SHEET

LO1 · Integrated GI/partial-GU history & exam

  • History (LOPQRSTA): Location, Onset, Provocation/Palliation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Timing, Associated sx + GI/GU ROS. Ask "what lives here?" by quadrant: RUQ = liver/gallbladder; LUQ = stomach/spleen; RLQ/LLQ = bowel + reproductive/ureter; epigastric = stomach/GERD/PUD/pancreas; suprapubic = bladder/pelvic.
  • Sequence — I-A-P-P: Inspect (contour, wall movement, scars, distention, hernias, masses, visible pulsation) → Auscultate before percussing/palpating (bowel sounds + aorta/renal/iliac arteries for bruits) → Percuss (4 quadrants + epigastric + suprapubic, then liver/spleen; tympany vs. dullness) → Palpate light→deep ×6 areas, painful area last.
  • Normal anchors: bowel sounds 5–34/min (<5 hypoactive, >34 hyperactive); liver span 6–12 cm MCL, edge soft/smooth, ≤3 cm below costal margin is normal (start palpation low if hepatomegaly suspected); spleen tip palpable in ~5% normal (Traube space tympany, Castell sign for new dullness on inspiration); aorta ≤3 cm.
  • Associate abnormal → disease: bruit + pulsatile mass → AAA; involuntary guarding (persists despite relaxation) → peritoneal irritation; loss of liver dullness/enlarged span → hepatomegaly; new splenic dullness → splenomegaly; friction rub → peritoneal/organ-surface pathology.

LO2 · Specialized GI/GU tests — perform & interpret

  • Murphy: palpate RUQ under liver edge during deep inspiration → pain/inspiratory arrest = cholecystitis.
  • Rovsing: press/release LLQ → pain referred to RLQ = appendicitis (referred rebound).
  • McBurney point: focal tenderness ~2 in. from ASIS toward umbilicus = appendicitis.
  • Psoas: resisted hip flexion (or extension in lateral decubitus) → RLQ pain = appendicitis (retrocecal).
  • Obturator: flex hip 90° + internally rotate → RLQ pain = appendicitis (pelvic).
  • Rebound tenderness / heel strike: pain on release, or pain from a transmitted heel-strike jolt = peritoneal irritation.
  • Shifting dullness: central tympany/flank dullness that shifts with rolling = ascites.
  • Hernia (ventral) test: bulge appears with head/leg lift or Valsalva (↑ intra-abdominal pressure) = abdominal wall hernia.
  • CVA tenderness: fist percussion over costovertebral angle → pain = renal/upper urinary tract irritation (pyelonephritis, stone).
  • Carnett sign (bonus): tense abdominal wall, re-palpate — pain unchanged/worse → abdominal wall source; pain improves → intra-abdominal source.

LO3 · Analyze findings → assessment, differential, plan

  • Assessment = single most-likely diagnosis + the specific history/exam facts supporting it.
  • Differential = defend alternatives (VINDICATE / "what lives here"); state what supports and what argues against each.
  • Plan = tests that resolve the actual uncertainty, then treatment/disposition matched to severity — don't repeat low-value painful maneuvers in an unstable/peritoneal patient.
  • Fast pattern discrimination: burning → GERD/PUD · RUQ+Murphy → cholecystitis · epigastric→back → pancreatitis · periumbilical→RLQ → appendicitis · LLQ+fever → diverticulitis · colicky+distention+obstipation → obstruction · pain ≫ exam findings → mesenteric ischemia.
  • Work-up anchors: CBC, CMP, UA, urine hCG (if pregnancy possible); RUQ → ultrasound first; CT with IV (not oral) contrast typical for acute abdomen; mesenteric ischemia → CT angiography urgently.

LO4 · Stigmata of chronic liver disease

  • Skin/vascular: spider angiomata, palmar erythema, jaundice/scleral icterus, caput medusae (portal HTN → collateral flow opens periumbilical veins → prominent radiating abdominal-wall veins).
  • Endocrine/systemic: gynecomastia, testicular atrophy, hair loss, clubbing.
  • Other clues: parotid enlargement (esp. alcohol-related disease), Dupuytren contracture. No single finding is specific — the constellation is what matters.
  • Ominous acute-abdomen companions: Grey Turner = flank ecchymosis (retroperitoneal bleed, e.g., severe pancreatitis); Cullen = periumbilical ecchymosis; Sister Mary Joseph nodule = firm umbilical nodule from metastatic carcinoma (stomach, colon, ovary, pancreas) — an ominous finding, not chronic liver disease itself but taught alongside it.
