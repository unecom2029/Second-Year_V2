Week 3 OMM - High-Yield One-Pager

Condensed by learning objective. Use this after the full pass.

LO1

Cervical: C7 most common (~70%), then C6 (~20%); C5/C8/T1 rare. C7 → triceps reflex/middle finger. C6 → biceps/brachioradialis/thumb-index. Mean age ~48, peak 50-54; more common in men.
Lumbar: L5 and S1 most common; L4-L5 and L5-S1 discs dominate. Posterolateral herniation hits the traversing root (below disc level); far-lateral hits the exiting root.

LO2

Root/spinal nerve: myotomal weakness crossing >1 peripheral nerve, dermatomal sensory loss with fuzzy borders, root-linked DTR loss (biceps C5-6, brachioradialis C6, triceps C7, patellar L3-4, Achilles S1), reproduced by Spurling/SLR/cough/Valsalva; palpation over the painful area is usually not reproducible.
Peripheral nerve: weakness limited to that nerve's muscles, sharp sensory border along a named territory, normal reflexes unless that arc is involved, reproduced by Tinel/compression/stretch at the entrapment site.

LO3

Pain: chemical inflammation, ischemia, mechanical deformation, DRG sensitization → sharp/electric, dermatomal.
Paresthesia: sensory axon irritation → tingling/burning/numbness in a root distribution.
Weakness: motor conduction block/axonal compromise → myotomal (triceps, wrist extension, dorsiflexion, plantarflexion).
Reflex change: decreased DTR = root level; hyperreflexia = cord/UMN. Pain/paresthesia with no objective weakness or reflex loss = radiculitis.

LO4

Radiculitis = inflamed/irritated root; pain/paresthesia, usually no objective deficit.
Radiculopathy = root disease with conduction block; pain plus objective sensory, motor, or reflex findings.
Myelopathy = spinal cord dysfunction; UMN signs — gait change, spasticity, hyperreflexia, clonus, Babinski/Hoffman's, hand clumsiness; needs urgent imaging/referral.
Peripheral neuropathy = named-nerve or stocking-glove pattern, not a single root.

LO5

Spondylosis = degenerative: disc desiccation/height loss, endplate change, facet/uncovertebral hypertrophy, ligament thickening, osteophytes → mechanically narrows canal/foramina; mechanical pain worse with loading/extension.
Spondyloarthropathy = inflammatory/autoimmune axial/enthesis disease (e.g., ankylosing spondylitis); morning stiffness that improves with movement, SI involvement, systemic clues (uveitis/psoriasis/IBD); imaging shows sacroiliitis/syndesmophytes, not a disc picture. OMT: gentle for spondylosis if no red flags; caution/avoid aggressive technique in fused or unstable spondyloarthropathy.

LO6

First localize: root, cord, local spine pain, hip/SI, muscle/tenderpoint, peripheral nerve, vascular, infection/malignancy, or emergent cauda equina — then match imaging/exam clues. Full ten-condition differential table is below (disc herniation, stenosis, spondylosis, spondylolysis, spondylolisthesis, spondyloarthropathy, psoas syndrome, piriformis syndrome, bursitis, cauda equina).

LO7

Discogenic: deep axial pain, may refer to buttock/thigh; provoked by sitting/flexion/Valsalva.
Facet joint: axial pain with referral (lumbar can mimic hip/SI); provoked by extension/rotation/Kemp's; no objective root deficit.
Myofascial: trigger-point referral that can mimic radicular pain; reproduced by direct trigger-point pressure; no myotomal/reflex deficit.
Radicular: dermatomal pain/paresthesia, often distal (below knee for lumbar); provoked by spine position, SLR, Spurling, cough/Valsalva; may show weakness, sensory loss, or reduced DTR.

LO8

Compression of multiple lumbosacral nerve roots in the canal — classically a large central disc herniation, severe stenosis, tumor, infection, hematoma, or trauma.
S2-S4 parasympathetic: urinary retention/overflow incontinence, bowel dysfunction.
S2-S5 sensory: saddle anesthesia/perineal loss.
Lumbosacral motor roots: bilateral weakness, gait difficulty, reduced reflexes.
Most concerning single symptom: new urinary retention. This is a same-day surgical emergency.

LO9

1. Triage red flags first (bowel/bladder, saddle anesthesia, fever/cancer/immunosuppression/IVDU, trauma, progressive weakness, myelopathic signs, unrelenting night pain).
2. Localize clinically: strength, sensation, DTRs, special tests, gait, OMM structural exam.
3. Conservative if uncomplicated: education, stay active, avoid bed rest >2 days, NSAIDs/acetaminophen, short-course muscle relaxant or neuropathic agent, PT/home exercise, close follow-up.
4. Selected pain control: glucocorticoids, NSAIDs/COX-2, ketorolac, tramadol/opioids selectively, gabapentin/pregabalin, TCA/SNRI, fluoroscopic epidural steroid injection.
5. OMT thoughtfully — gentle indirect/soft tissue/CS/FPR/BLT/traction; avoid aggressive direct technique in acute neuro compromise.
6. Escalate for red flags, progressive deficit, myelopathy, cauda equina, infection/malignancy, or a failed conservative trial.

LO10

Cauda equina: saddle anesthesia, urinary retention/incontinence, bowel dysfunction, bilateral severe symptoms.
Progressive deficit: worsening weakness, atrophy, can't heel/toe walk or squat-and-rise.
Myelopathy/UMN: gait abnormality, spasticity, hyperreflexia, clonus, Babinski, Hoffman's, Lhermitte's, fine motor decline.
Infection/tumor: fever, chills, weight loss, night/rest pain, immunosuppression, cancer history, IVDU.
Trauma/instability: significant trauma, osteoporosis/steroid use, step-off deformity, focal vertebral tenderness.
Non-spine emergency: abdominal pain, dyspnea, vascular symptoms, mechanics-mismatched pain.

LO11

Do not image every uncomplicated acute radic case without red flags. Image early for red flags, progressive deficit, or persistent symptoms when intervention is being considered.
X-ray: trauma, fracture, alignment/instability (flexion-extension views), oblique for foraminal narrowing.
MRI w/o contrast: preferred for disc herniation, stenosis, root compression, myelopathy — most nontraumatic radiculopathy; correlate clinically since degenerative findings are common in asymptomatic patients.
MRI w/ contrast: cancer/infection concern, postop scar vs. recurrent disc, inflammatory/mass process.
CT: bony detail, acute trauma, MRI unavailable/contraindicated.
CT myelogram: MRI contraindicated/equivocal, or to clarify root impingement.
EMG/NCS: unclear localization/chronicity or root vs. peripheral neuropathy; needle EMG adds root-level info beyond NCS alone.

LO12

OMT is appropriate for stable somatic dysfunction with mild, non-progressive radicular symptoms and a normal neuro exam. It is not a substitute for emergency workup when progressive deficit, bowel/bladder change, saddle anesthesia, myelopathy signs, fracture, malignancy, infection, severe osteoporosis, or instability are present. Full indications/contraindications table is below.

LO13

Goal: treat what's safe/treatable — secondary somatic dysfunction, muscle guarding, tenderpoints, fascial/ligamentous tension, mechanics aggravating foraminal load. OMT does not decompress a surgical emergency.
Sequence: triage/consent (stop if symptoms peripheralize) → baseline exam (pain distribution, strength, sensation, DTRs, provocative tests, gait, structural exam) → low-risk technique first (soft tissue, counterstrain, FPR, BLT, gentle ME, traction/decompression) → prefer centralization/ease, stop if symptoms worsen/spread distally → treat related regions (cervical: thoracic outlet, ribs, scalenes, upper thoracic, OA/AA if safe; lumbar: pelvis/sacrum, psoas/piriformis, paraspinals, nerve-root decompression FPR) → recheck neuro status and give safety-net instructions. Avoid cervical thrust/compression into a Spurling-like position or lumbar HVLA that peripheralizes pain with any progressive deficit.

LO14

Eight named tests to recognize by vignette: Spurling's, Lhermitte's, straight leg raise, step-off deformity, lumbar spinous percussion, Hoffman's, clonus/hyperreflexia, and Babinski. Spurling and SLR confirm root tension; Lhermitte's/Hoffman's/clonus/Babinski are cord/UMN red flags; step-off and percussion screen for instability/fracture/infection. Full how-to-perform and meaning table is below.

LO15

Still = position into ease → add compression/traction → move through neutral toward the barrier.
FPR = neutral/idle position → add facilitating force → move into ease, hold seconds.
Counterstrain = find tenderpoint → position of comfort until tenderness drops.
Muscle Energy (ME) = engage barrier → isometric contraction → relax → take up slack, repeat.
Full region-by-region atlas (OA, AA, lower cervical, seated/supine lumbar Type 1 & 2, sacrum, anterior cervical/lumbar tenderpoints, psoas, piriformis, lumbar nerve-root decompression) is below.
