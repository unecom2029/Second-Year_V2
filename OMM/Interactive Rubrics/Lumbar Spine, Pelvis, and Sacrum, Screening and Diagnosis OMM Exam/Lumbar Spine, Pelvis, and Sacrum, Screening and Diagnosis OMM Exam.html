Lumbar Spine, Pelvis, and Sacrum — Screening and Diagnosis
1COMMUNICATION: Appropriately communicates to patient about the physical exam being done (uses lay terms, speaks to pt, assures understanding).
2Obtains consent before doing the exam AND again with any particularly sensitive parts of the exam.
3WASHES HANDS: Prior to technique (may do at beginning of encounter) – hand sanitizer, or at least 20s with soap and water (if student is cued to do this task, then no credit will be given).
4LUMBAR SPINE SCREENING: Effectively segmentally screens the entire lumbar spine (L1-L5) with appropriate contact and force vector bilaterally.
5LUMBAR SPINE SEGMENTAL DIAGNOSIS: Correctly performs a 3-positional segmental diagnosis of the most restricted lumbar (L1-L5) segment AND is accurate with the diagnosis.
6INNOMINATE SCREENING: Standing Flexion Test is performed correctly (correct landmarks, patient instructions, and procedure) AND Positive side is correctly identified.
7Supine exam performed correctly (reseats pelvis, evaluates asymmetry of ASIS/pubic tubercles, ASIS or SI compression test).
8Prone exam performed correctly (accurately identifies asymmetry in PSIS).
9Accuracy of Innominate Diagnosis.
10SACRUM SCREENING: Seated Flexion Test is performed correctly (feet on floor, correct landmarks, patient instructions, and procedure) AND Positive side/Axis is correctly identified.
11Prone exam performed correctly (boney landmarks correct, identifies asymmetry at sacral base/ILA statically and dynamically).
12Spring AND Sphinx Tests are performed correctly (patient instruction, correct landmarks, appropriate force) AND are correct with interpretation (positive or negative).
13Accuracy of Sacrum Diagnosis.
