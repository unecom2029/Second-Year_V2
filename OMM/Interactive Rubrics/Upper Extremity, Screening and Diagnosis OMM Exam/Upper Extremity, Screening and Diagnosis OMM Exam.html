Upper Extremity — Screening and Diagnosis
1COMMUNICATION: Appropriately communicates to patient about the physical exam being done (uses lay terms, speaks to pt, assures understanding).
2Obtains consent before doing the exam AND again with any particularly sensitive parts of the exam.
3WASHES HANDS: Prior to technique (may do at beginning of encounter) – hand sanitizer, or at least 20s with soap and water (if student is cued to do this task, then no credit will be given).
4DEMONSTRATES CORRECT SEQUENCE/PROCEDURE FOR UPPER EXTREMITY SCREENING EXAM: Accurately evaluates the SC joint for restriction in anterior/posterior, superior/inferior restriction in motion bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
5Accurately evaluates the AC joint for restriction in external/internal rotation bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
6Accurately evaluates the elbow for restriction in flexion/extension, abduction/adduction (valgus/varus) bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
7Accurately evaluates the radial head for restriction in posterior/anterior glide with pronation/supination bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
8Accurately evaluates the wrist for restriction in flexion/extension, abduction/adduction bilaterally.
9Accurately evaluates the wrist for restriction in the radiocarpal joint and carpal bones bilaterally.
10Accurately evaluates the hand for restriction in the carpal bones, metacarpals, and phalanges bilaterally.
11ACCURATELY IDENTIFIES THE MOST RESTRICTED SOMATIC DYSFUNCTION OF THE UPPER EXTREMITY: Correctly performs a positional diagnosis of the most restricted somatic dysfunction in the upper extremity.
12Accuracy of the Specific Upper Extremity Diagnosis.
