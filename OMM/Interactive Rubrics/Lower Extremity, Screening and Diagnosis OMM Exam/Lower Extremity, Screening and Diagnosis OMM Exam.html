Lower Extremity — Screening and Diagnosis
1COMMUNICATION: Appropriately communicates to patient about the physical exam being done (uses lay terms, speaks to pt, assures understanding).
2Obtains consent before doing the exam AND again with any particularly sensitive parts of the exam.
3WASHES HANDS: Prior to technique (may do at beginning of encounter) – hand sanitizer, or at least 20s with soap and water (if student is cued to do this task, then no credit will be given).
4DEMONSTRATES CORRECT SEQUENCE/PROCEDURE FOR LOWER EXTREMITY SCREENING EXAM: Accurately evaluates hip in flexion/extension, internal/external rotation, abduction/adduction bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
5Accurately evaluates the knee in flexion/extension, internal/external rotation, abduction/adduction (valgus/varus), anterior/posterior glide bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
6Accurately evaluates the fibular head for anterolateral/posteromedial restrictions bilaterally, AND is able to discriminate between articular or soft tissue barriers.
7Accurately evaluates the talocrural and subtalar joints of the foot for dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, and eversion bilaterally.
8Accurately evaluates the navicular, cuboid, and cuneiforms for restricted motion bilaterally.
9Accurately evaluates the metatarsals and phalanges for restricted motion bilaterally.
10ACCURATELY IDENTIFIES THE MOST RESTRICTED SOMATIC DYSFUNCTION OF THE LOWER EXTREMITY: Correctly performs a positional diagnosis of the most restricted somatic dysfunction in the lower extremity.
