Murmurs & maneuvers

  • Systolic = AS or MR · diastolic = MS or AR. Stenosis = gradient · regurgitation = extra volume.
  • Thick wall = concentric (AS) · big chamber = eccentric (AR, MR). MS spares the LV.
  • MS: LA > LV in diastole · rumble + opening snap at apex · rheumatic · AF, LA clot, hemoptysis, pregnancy unmasks.
  • AS: LV ≫ aorta in systole (200/25 vs 110/70) · crescendo–decrescendo R 2nd ICS → carotids · parvus et tardus · LA always high · bicuspid (40–60) vs calcific (70+) · exercise intolerance first, then angina, syncope, HF · avoid nitrates.
  • MR: tall v wave · holosystolic apex → axilla · MVP = click + late systolic murmur · posteromedial papillary muscle after inferior MI.
  • AR: wide pulse pressure (160/60) · early diastolic decrescendo, LSB · water-hammer pulses · Austin Flint.
  • Standing/Valsalva (↓preload): HCM and MVP louder, others softer · squat/leg raise: opposite · handgrip (↑afterload): MR, AR, VSD louder; AS and HCM softer · inspiration: right-sided louder.
  • Innocent: soft (<3/6), midsystolic, positional. Diastolic is almost always pathologic.
