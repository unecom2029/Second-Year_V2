🔑 Two questions solve most vignettes

1. Systolic or diastolic? In systole the aortic valve should be open and the mitral closed, so a systolic murmur is AS or MR. In diastole it's the reverse, so a diastolic murmur is MS or AR.
2. Pressure or volume? A stenosis creates a gradient across the valve. A regurgitant valve dumps volume into the chamber behind it: a tall v wave in the LA (MR), or a dilated LV with a wide pulse pressure (AR).

Q: Which two lesions produce a systolic murmur, and which hemodynamic sign separates them?

Answer

AS and MR. AS has an LV–aortic systolic gradient (with blunted carotids). MR has a tall LA v wave with no gradient between the LV and aorta.

Q: Which two lesions produce a diastolic murmur, and which hemodynamic sign separates them?

Answer

MS and AR. MS has an LA–LV diastolic gradient (opening snap, rumble). AR has a wide pulse pressure with bounding pulses.

Q: In which lesion is the LV small and underfilled rather than hypertrophied or dilated?

Answer

Mitral stenosis. The obstruction is upstream of the LV, so the LV is protected and underfilled while the LA takes the strain.
