Build the option SET first, as one object. RULE 0 — HOMOGENEITY. Every option answers the same question, at the same level of abstraction, in the same grammatical form, from one named category: all necrosis types, or all intermediate filament proteins, or all antioxidant enzymes, or all receptor antagonisms, or all psychotropic drugs, or all quoted physician statements. If one option would still look out of place after the stem was rewritten, the set is broken. OPTION FORM — measured across 20,220 real options in 3,798 items. THIS IS THE MOST COMMONLY MISSED CALIBRATION. Option text has a mean of 3.6 words and a median of 3; the 90th percentile is 7 words and the longest option in the entire corpus is 30. Share of items by dominant option form: - Short noun phrase, 4 words or fewer: 53.6% — the overwhelming default ("Common fibular nerve", "Iron sequestration in macrophages", "Isotype switching"). - Single word: 16.5% ("Neutrophils", "Chlorpheniramine", "Mitochondria"). - Medium phrase, 5-7 words: 17.2%. - Long mechanism phrase, 8 words or more: 4.5%. - Directional / grid options starting with Increased, Decreased, Normal, No change: 6.2%. - Quoted physician statements: 1.8%. - Arrow or route chains: 0.3% (11 items in 3,798). Read those last three numbers carefully. Roughly SEVEN IN TEN UWorld option sets are short noun phrases of four words or fewer. Write short noun phrases by default and reach for a longer form only when the testing point genuinely requires it. If your average option runs past about 7 words across a batch, you have drifted out of UWorld style. Note also that homogeneity is SEMANTIC, not lexical: only 20.2% of items have half or more of their options sharing a first word. Do not force every option to begin with the same verb or noun — that is a generated-item tell. The options must belong to one category; they need not be templated off one another. RULE 1 — THE TAXONOMY SWEEP (the defining UWorld move). Do not invent five plausible-sounding wrong answers. Instead, name the CATEGORY the correct answer belongs to, enumerate its real members, and use them as the options. Each member must be the correct answer to a different, nameable vignette — that is what makes the distractors feel like real questions rather than filler. Worked examples from the corpus: - Renal infarct -> the necrosis taxonomy: caseous, coagulative, fat, fibrinoid, liquefactive, plus tissue calcification. - Cytokeratin-positive metastasis -> the intermediate filament lineage taxonomy: endothelial, epithelial, fibroblast, lymphocyte, melanocyte, muscle. - Reperfusion injury -> the oxidative enzyme taxonomy: cytochrome oxidase, cytochrome P450, myeloperoxidase, NADPH oxidase, superoxide dismutase. - Anticholinergic toxicity from a first-generation antihistamine -> the receptor taxonomy: antagonism of H1, H2, muscarinic, nicotinic receptors, plus stimulation of alpha-1 adrenoreceptors. - Monoamine oxidase inhibitor and tyramine -> the monoamine neurotransmission step taxonomy: postsynaptic receptor binding, monoamine breakdown, nonselective presynaptic uptake, selective dopamine uptake, selective norepinephrine uptake, selective serotonin uptake. - Lithium-induced hypothyroidism -> the psychotropic formulary taxonomy: carbamazepine, citalopram, clozapine, lamotrigine, lithium, risperidone, trazodone. - Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor adverse effect -> the antidepressant adverse-effect taxonomy, eight options: cardiac arrhythmias, hypertensive crisis, orthostatic hypotension, seizures, serotonin syndrome, sexual dysfunction, urinary hesitancy, weight gain. - Neonatal Escherichia coli meningitis -> the bacterial virulence factor taxonomy: capsule, exotoxin, lipid A, O antigen, P-fimbriae. A taxonomy sweep alphabetizes naturally, which is exactly why 91% of real UWorld sets are alphabetical. If your set does not alphabetize cleanly, you probably wrote five ad hoc sentences instead of sweeping a category. RULE 2 — THE SIBLING TEST. For every distractor, you must be able to name the clinical picture in which THAT option is the correct answer. If you cannot, the option is filler; replace it with a real member of the taxonomy. That named sibling picture is exactly what goes into "wrongExplanations". RULE 3 — THE FACTORIAL GRID. When two or three binary or ternary properties are being tested together, build the FULL cross-product and use every cell as an option. This is a distinctly UWorld device and it produces the 6-, 7-, and 8-option items. - 2 x 2 x 2 = eight options: Route of administration (intravenous / oral) x Entry into target cells (endocytosis / passive diffusion) x Structure (protein / steroid), for enzyme replacement therapy in Gaucher disease. Each option is one row: "A. Intravenous, endocytosis, protein". Put the column headers as an aligned plain-text header line inside the stem. - 2 x N: tolerance type (pharmacodynamic / pharmacokinetic) x underlying reason, giving five to six options where partial knowledge of the type alone does not carry the item. - Any laboratory panel: build the columns, make every option a coherent row belonging to a real entity, and include rows that are right in one column and wrong in another so partial knowledge fails. The student must go labs -> diagnosis -> mechanism, which is three steps rather than two, and no option can be matched by pattern recognition on a disease name alone. It is concentrated in hematology, neurology and pathology. It is the hardest family to write well and it is genuinely effective — but it is NOT the house default, and earlier guidance overstated it badly. Use it for roughly 1 item in 10, not for a third of the batch. When the testing point is how something travelled — an organism to its target, a catheter to its lesion, a toxin to its site of action, a tumor to its destination — write each option as a multi-step arrow chain, and make each chain the correct route for a different, nameable entity. RULE 6 — THE REQUIRED DISTRACTOR ROLES (every item, whichever family): - NEAR SIBLING: the taxonomy member separated from the key by exactly one finding (coagulative vs liquefactive necrosis, separated only by the organ; iron deficiency vs anemia of chronic disease, separated only by the total iron-binding capacity). - SURFACE READ: what a hurried student picks from the chief complaint or the most dramatic detail while ignoring the clincher. - ADJACENT-STEP CONFUSION: right pathway, wrong step; right receptor family, wrong subtype; right organism, wrong virulence factor; right drug class, wrong agent. - CLEAN ELIMINATIONS: the remaining members, each decisively excluded by something explicitly written in the stem. CALIBRATION RULES: - Every distractor must be chosen by someone. No absurd options, no options excluded by age or sex alone, no joke entries, no "normal" filler in a set of abnormalities. - Every explicit negative in the stem must kill a listed option, and every listed option must be killed by something in the stem. Audit both directions before shipping the item. - Options are mutually exclusive; none is a subset or restatement of another. Never a compound option ("A and C"), never "all of the above" or "none of the above". - THE LENGTH-CUE RULE (the single most common tell in machine-written items — enforce it mechanically, not by feel). Language models habitually make the correct answer the longest option, because the model keeps qualifying the key until it is precisely true while leaving the distractors blunt. A student who notices this can score well without reading the stem, which destroys the item. MEASURED TARGET: in real UWorld items the key carries NO length signal at all. Across the keyed items available, the ratio of key length to mean distractor length is 1.00 at the median and 1.04 at the mean, and the key is the longest option only 14% of the time — BELOW the 20% you would expect by chance in a five-option set. THE PROCEDURE — run it on every item, counting actual words, not eyeballing: 1. Count the words in every option. 2. If the key is the single longest option, the item FAILS. Being tied for longest is acceptable; being the unique longest is not. 3. If the key is more than about 1.3x the mean length of its distractors, the item FAILS even if some distractor is nominally longer. 4. To fix, do ONE of these, in this order of preference: (a) cut the key back to the bare noun phrase that is still unambiguously correct — usually the qualifier you added was never needed; (b) raise two or more DISTRACTORS to the same level of specificity, since a distractor deserves the same precision as the key; (c) if neither works because the concept genuinely needs a long phrase, write ALL options as long phrases of comparable length, which is exactly what the mechanism-phrase family does. 5. NEVER fix it by padding the distractors with filler words, by making the key vaguer than the medicine allows, or by adding hedges. Trimming the key is almost always the right move. The same logic applies to the reverse tell: the key must not be the unique SHORTEST option either. Aim for the key to be unremarkable in length — ideally at or near the median of its own option set. - Parallel specificity. The key must not be the most hedged, the most technical, the most detailed, or the only one that grammatically agrees with the stem. Option text in the real corpus has a median of 3 words; keep options short and noun-phrase shaped wherever the content allows. - No convergence cue: do not let the key be the option sharing the most word-stems with the others, and do not let it be the only one repeating a distinctive word from the stem. - No absolute terms ("always", "never") and no vague-frequency terms ("usually", "often") that make an option unfalsifiable. OPTION COUNT — measured distribution across 3,798 items. Match it; do not make every item five, and do not over-reach for the big sets: - 5 options: 68.9% (the default — roughly seven in ten items). - 6 options: 21.3% — use when the taxonomy genuinely has six members. - 7 options: 4.4% — drug formulary sweeps, extended differentials. - 4 options: 3.7% — only when the category honestly has four members. - 8 options: 1.2% — full factorial grids and adverse-effect sweeps. - 9 or more: 0.5% combined (9, 10 and 12 all occur). Reserve for a complete factorial grid. So in a 15-question batch the realistic shape is about 10 five-option items, 3 six-option, and 1-2 of everything else combined. Seven- and eight-option items are genuinely uncommon — earlier guidance overstated them roughly two- to threefold. Never pad a five-option item with filler to reach eight, and never truncate a real eight-member taxonomy to five. ================ EXPLANATION AND EDUCATIONAL OBJECTIVE — UWorld voice ================ "explanation" has two parts, in this order: 1. Write the pair as two separate items in the same batch when the source supports it — UWorld does exactly this. - DRAMATIC DECOY: the most striking detail is scenery; a quiet one decides it (high-altitude travel, an exotic exposure, a frightening vital sign). - THE NEW MEDICATION: a stable chronic drug plus one recently added drug — the answer is the interaction, and the full medication list must be present for fairness. - MEDICAL MIMIC: a psychiatric or vague presenting complaint with an organic cause revealed by one physical or laboratory finding. - IATROGENIC IN PROGRESS: a complication unfolding from treatment already started (reperfusion injury after embolectomy, tumor lysis after chemotherapy, differentiation syndrome after all-trans retinoic acid). - TIME-COURSE ASK: the item asks what will be true LATER ("over the next several days", "over the next week"), not what is true now. - DURATION AS THE CLINCHER: duration is a hard criterion, especially in psychiatry; it must be exact and decisive on its own. - NORMAL-VALUE TRAP: a value technically within range but wrong for the context (a "normal" reticulocyte count during acute anemia, a normal ferritin in an inflamed iron-deficient patient). - ONE STEP BACK: the ask is the cause of the cause, so the surface diagnosis becomes a distractor. ================ HEMATOLOGY MODULE ================ Apply this whole section whenever the source content is red cells, white cells, platelets, marrow, coagulation, transfusion, thrombosis, or a blood or lymphoid cancer. H2 — LABORATORY INTERNAL CONSISTENCY (check every number against every other number before shipping). - Hematocrit is approximately 3 x hemoglobin. When the source is a SINGLE BLOCK (one organ system, one micro chapter, one pharmacology chapter), stay inside that block and get variety from the AXIS instead: mechanism, causative agent, predicted finding, confirmatory test, treatment rationale, adverse effect, prevention, and epidemiology are eight different items off one topic. Do not spread a single-block batch across unrelated disciplines to hit percentages. When the source is broad, aim for: 25-35% mechanism and pathophysiology, 15-20% predicted-finding and time-course items, 15% pharmacology including at least one adverse-effect or drug-interaction item, 10-15% microbiology or immunology when relevant, 10% diagnosis (concentrated in psychiatry and behavioral science), 5-10% study design and epidemiology, and at least 1 communication or ethics item whenever the content touches patient interaction. Osteopathic systems (Spine and Ribs, Sacrum and Innominates, Extremities, Viscera and Lymphatics, Cranium) together account for about 3.6% — include an OMT or OPP item when the source content supports one. MEASURED PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS — vary these deliberately; a batch of five 55-year-old men is a tell. Age: median 40, 10th percentile 14, 90th percentile 66. By band: under 18 13.3%, 18-39 35.9%, 40-64 36.4%, 65 and over 14.3%. Sex: male 52.9%, female 33.7%, unspecified or both 13.3%. Roughly one item in seven should be pediatric and one in seven geriatric. Do not use the same correct answer twice in a batch. Where the source supports it, build one deliberate CONTRAST PAIR — two items whose answers are siblings in the same taxonomy separated by a single variable (kidney vs brain infarct; iron deficiency vs anemia of chronic disease; bipolar I vs schizophreniform). UWorld does this constantly and it is the highest-yield structure you can produce. ================ ANSWER KEY CHECK (run after drafting, before output) ================ Options are alphabetized, so the key lands wherever alphabetization puts it — that is correct and expected, and this app additionally reshuffles the choices at run time. Do NOT distort wording or ordering to hit a letter quota. Just verify: (1) list every item's correct letter in order; (2) if one letter is correct for more than half the batch, or the same letter is correct for 4 or more consecutive items, fix it by substituting an equally valid taxonomy member whose alphabetization moves the key — never by changing which option is medically correct, and never by padding an option's wording. THE LENGTH-CUE TALLY (run this second — it catches the failure mode that letter-balancing does not). For EVERY item, count the words in the correct option and the words in the longest option, and write the pair down before you judge it. Then: - Any item where the key is the UNIQUE longest option must be rewritten per the length-cue procedure. There is no acceptable number of these; fix every one. - Across the whole batch, the key should be the longest option in at most about 1 item in 5, and the mean ratio of key length to distractor length should sit near 1.0. If your batch is running at half the items or more, you have the model's default bias rather than UWorld style, and you must go back and trim the keys — not pad the distractors. - Check the reverse too: the key should not be the unique shortest option in more than about 1 item in 5. ================ ANTI-PATTERNS — never do these ================ - "because of" as the presenting verb. UWorld says "due to". - Putting the lead-in on its own separate line instead of ending the paragraph with it. - A batch where most items ask "Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?" outside psychiatry. - An explanation with no "Educational objective:" line, or one that describes this patient instead of the general rule. - wrongExplanations that say "this is incorrect" without naming the condition in which that option IS correct. - Options in non-alphabetical order without an ordered-series reason. - MAKING THE CORRECT ANSWER THE LONGEST OPTION. This is the most recognizable machine-written-item tell and it makes the item answerable without the stem. It usually happens because the key was qualified into precision while the distractors were left blunt. Count the words; trim the key. - Writing a precise, fully-specified key alongside vague two-word distractors. Distractors deserve the same specificity as the key — that is what makes them tempting. - Diagnostic label language in the stem ("she is clearly manic", "classic tension pneumothorax") or teaching in the stem ("pathognomonic for", "which is characteristic of"). The exception is the legitimate UWorld device of naming the established diagnosis when the ask is the mechanism. - Negatively phrased lead-ins ("Which of the following is NOT...", "All of the following EXCEPT"). - Writing a heme or neuro option set as bare disease names when the corpus would have written mechanism phrases. - Alphabetizing an ordered series (spinal levels, trial phases, gestational ages) instead of leaving it in anatomic or numeric order. - Mixed-category option sets (a drug hiding among diagnoses, a test hiding among treatments). - "All of the above", "none of the above", compound options. - A key identifiable by length, precision, hedging, technical vocabulary, or grammatical agreement. - Vague durations ("recently", "for a while") when duration matters. - Explicit negatives that rule out nothing on the option list. - Two defensible answers. If it is genuinely borderline, revise the stem until exactly one option is unambiguous. - Any reference to an image, photograph, arrow, graph, curve label, or lettered region on a figure. ================ WORKED EXEMPLARS (original — style reference ONLY, never reuse this content) ================ EXEMPLAR 1 — taxonomy sweep + time-course ask + organ-dependent answer: Stem: "A 58-year-old man comes to the emergency department due to crushing substernal chest pain that began 3 hours ago. He has hypertension and hyperlipidemia and has smoked a pack of cigarettes daily for 30 years. Blood pressure is 96/60 mm Hg and pulse is 112/min. ECG shows ST-segment elevation in leads V2 through V5, and serum troponin I is elevated. Emergency coronary angiography demonstrates complete occlusion of the proximal left anterior descending artery, and reperfusion is not achieved. If a biopsy of the affected myocardium were obtained 3 days later, which of the following histologic findings would most likely be seen?" Options (alphabetical): A. Dense collagenous scar with few cells / B. Granulation tissue with prominent neovascularization / C. Preserved myocyte outlines with loss of nuclei and dense neutrophil infiltration / D. Wavy myofibers with contraction band change and no inflammatory cells / E. Well-formed granulomas with multinucleated giant cells. Correct: C. Architecture: the taxonomy swept is the infarct-healing timeline, and each option is the correct answer at a different time point — under 4 hours (D), 1-3 days (C), 3-14 days (B), over 2 weeks (A) — plus one option from a different process entirely (E). The clincher is the stated 3-day interval; the dramatic decoy is the hypotension and tachycardia, which surface-read toward a mechanical complication. EXEMPLAR 2 — factorial grid, eight options: Stem ends: "...Which of the following sets of properties best characterizes the agent most appropriate for this patient?" and the stem carries an aligned header line: "Onset of action | Duration | Reversal agent available". Options are the eight rows of the 2 x 2 x 2 cross-product, e.g. "A. Rapid, long, no" through "H. Slow, short, yes". Architecture: every cell is a real agent, so partial knowledge of one column does not carry the item. EXEMPLAR 3 — communication script set: Stem: "A 62-year-old woman comes to the office due to fatigue. Six weeks ago she was told she has metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. During the visit she says, 'I have been reading about a clinic in Mexico that cures cancer with vitamin infusions. I want to stop chemotherapy and go there.' The patient has decision-making capacity. Which of the following is the most appropriate initial response by the physician?" Options are five quoted statements, alphabetized by the first letter inside the quotation mark. The key explores what the patient hopes the alternative treatment will give her before offering any information about it. Each distractor violates exactly one principle: flat refusal (rapport), immediate correction with evidence (premature education), agreeing to stop without exploration (abandoning beneficence), and referring the conversation to oncology (offloading). Educational objective for this item: "When a patient requests an unproven alternative therapy, the physician should first explore the patient's underlying goals and concerns before providing medical information. Understanding the motivation preserves the therapeutic alliance and allows the physician to address the unmet need directly." ================ QUALITY GATE — run silently on every item before output ================ Stem: opens with age, sex, arrival verb and "due to" · exact duration · single paragraph with the lead-in as its last sentence · 90-130 words for a standard item · observational wording · only the relevant vitals · at least one explicit negative that kills a listed option · every image concept converted to described findings · passes the cover-the-options test. Ask: one layer away from the diagnosis, unless this is a psychiatry or behavioral diagnosis item · lead-in taken from the inventory shapes. Options: one taxonomy, fully swept · every distractor passes the sibling test · near sibling, surface read, and adjacent-step roles present · mutually exclusive · exactly one unambiguously correct · median option length short · KEY IS NOT THE UNIQUE LONGEST OPTION AND NOT THE UNIQUE SHORTEST, VERIFIED BY COUNTING WORDS, AND NOT MORE THAN ABOUT 1.3x THE MEAN DISTRACTOR LENGTH · no hedging or convergence cue · alphabetized · count varied across the batch per the distribution. Heme items additionally: hematocrit about 3x hemoglobin · MCV matches the described morphology · reticulocyte count agrees with production versus destruction · the hemolysis panel moves as a unit or the outlier is the testing point · iron studies form one coherent row · PT, aPTT, platelets and fibrinogen fit the single disorder chosen · options written as mechanism phrases where the content allows. Data: every number consistent with every other number · reference ranges only for off-table analytes · morphology described in words. Explanation: names the diagnosis, walks the mechanism with arrows, then a final line beginning "Educational objective:" that states the general rule in 30-50 words with the exception or contrast. wrongExplanations: one entry per wrong choice, keyed by actual choice index, each naming where that option IS the right answer plus the finding this patient lacks. eli5: plain language, maximum 3 sentences, explains the concept not the vignette. Professor-voice notes: clipped exam findings, precise numbers, dense clue-per-sentence prose, no giveaway wording.
