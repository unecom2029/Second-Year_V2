🎯 Hematology Summative 3 — High-Yield One-Pager

Condensed by objective cluster — hematopoiesis · anemia I & II · the laboratory · benign white cells · bleeding · clotting · transfusion · pharmacology · sepsis · HIV · malignancy · antineoplastics · lymphatics · VTE. Covers all 83 study guide objectives — 82 worked in full, plus prescription writing, the one objective with no lecture source.

Hematopoiesis · LO 2 · 81 · 83

Sites & hierarchy

  • Yolk sac → fetal liver & spleen → bone marrow. Adult: vertebrae, ribs, sternum, pelvis (axial).
  • HSC = self-renewal + differentiation; multipotent. Over-self-renewal = leukemia.
  • Myeloid: RBC, megakaryocyte→platelet, neutrophil, mono→macrophage, eos, baso. Lymphoid: T (thymus), B (marrow)→plasma cell, NK.
  • Platelet = anucleate membrane fragment → aspirin lasts its 7–10 day life.
  • Extramedullary hematopoiesis + skull/face marrow expansion → frontal bossing in thalassemia.

Hormones → drugs

  • EPO (kidney) → RBC · TPO → platelets · G-CSF/GM-CSF → granulocytes · GH/IGF → lymphocytes.
  • Retic low = production problem. Retic high = loss/destruction.

Marrow biopsy & CBC

  • Prone, posterior superior iliac spine. Aspirate = cytology; core = architecture/cellularity.
  • Hypercellular → leukemia or compensatory drive. Hypocellular → aplastic anemia, MDS, post-chemo.
  • Do NOT biopsy for routine anemia.
  • Hct ≈ 3 × Hgb. Female 37–47%, male 42–52%. RDW ↑ = two populations.
  • Acute hemorrhage: initial Hgb can be normal — concentration unchanged.
Anemia I · LO 18 · 19 · 20 · 29 · 42 · 53 · 55

The 3 buckets + the algorithm

  • Blood loss · impaired production · increased destruction.
  • Confirm → read MCV first → retic count → targeted study → smear.
  • <80 micro · 80–100 normo · >100 macro.

Iron deficiency

  • Fe ↓, ferritin ↓, TIBC ↑, TSAT <15%, MCV ↓, marrow iron absent.
  • Stations: duodenum (Fe²⁺ only) → transferrin → cells → ferritin.
  • Adult male or postmenopausal female = GI blood loss until proven otherwise → colonoscopy.
  • Ferritin is an acute phase reactant — can be falsely normal.
  • Small AND pale: extra divisions before maturation.

B12 · pernicious · folate

  • B12 = DNA synthesis + myelin. Folate = DNA only. B12 has the Brain.
  • Megaloblastic: macro-ovalocytes, hypersegmented neutrophils (≥6 lobes), hypercellular marrow, retic low, LDH high = ineffective erythropoiesis.
  • MMA elevated in B12, normal in folate. Homocysteine ↑ in both.
  • PA = autoimmune loss of parietal cells → no IF. Anti-parietal 85–90% (sensitive), anti-IF 75% (specific). Triad: anemia + gastric atrophy (cancer risk) + subacute combined degeneration.
  • Path: pepsin → haptocorrin → pancreatic proteases → IF → terminal ileum → transcobalamin II.
  • Drug causes: metformin (ileum), PPI (stomach).
  • Never folate alone — corrects the count while the cord degenerates.
  • Folate in pregnancy: neural tube closes at ~4 weeks — supplement before conception.
The laboratory · LO 27 · 53

Measured vs calculated

  • Measured: RBC + MCV (impedance), Hgb (SLS photometry, 555 nm).
  • Calculated: Hct = RBC×MCV/10 · MCH = Hgb/RBC×10 · MCHC = Hgb/Hct×100 · RDW = SD/MCV.
  • Ranges: RBC 3.5–5.5 ×10⁶ · MCV 80–100 fL (lower in children) · MCHC 31–37 · RDW 11.4–14.5 · retics 0.8–4%.
  • Hct ≈ 3 × Hgb, only if the MCV is normal. Breaking the rule is itself a finding.
  • EDTA (lavender) ≤3 h ideal, ≤8 h acceptable. Citrate is the fallback for platelets.

Interferences

  • MCHC >37 g/dL = artefact — except hereditary spherocytosis.
  • Cold agglutinins: ↓RBC, ↑MCV, ↑↑MCHC → warm to 37 °C.
  • Microcytes/schistocytes/WBC fragments → falsely HIGH platelets. Clumps/giant platelets → falsely LOW → citrate tube or PLT-F channel.
  • Lipemia, huge WBC, bilirubin → falsely high Hgb. nRBCs → falsely high WBC.

The smear

  • Read the monolayer — the feather edge manufactures spherocytes.
  • Spherocyte → HS / autoimmune · target → thalassemia, HbC, liver, splenectomy · tear-drop → myelofibrosis · acanthocyte (few, irregular) → liver · echinocyte (many, even) → uremia/artefact · schistocyte → microangiopathy.
  • Target cells → send hemoglobin electrophoresis. Morphology cannot call thalassemia.
  • Malaria: fever + travel + thrombocytopenia. Report parasitaemia %. P. falciparum >10%, infects all ages; vivax/ovale form hypnozoites.

Anemia of inflammation

  • Most common anemia in hospitalized US patients. Ferritin ↑, TIBC ↓.
  • IL-6 → hepcidin ↑ → degrades ferroportin → iron locked in macrophages/enterocytes. Plus ↓EPO, ↓erythroid proliferation, ↑hemophagocytosis.
  • Treat the underlying disease. Oral iron does not work; IV iron bypasses the locked door.

Anemia II · LO 5 · 7 · 25 · 28 · 51

Intravascular vs extravascular

  • Shared: short lifespan · ↑ unconjugated bilirubin · compensatory retic ↑.
  • Intravascular triad: pink plasma · dark urine · absent haptoglobin. Schistocytes. Kidney at risk — free heme is nephrotoxic.
  • Extravascular: spherocytes, splenomegaly, haptoglobin only mildly low.

Match the cell

  • Ball → spherocytosis · Bite → G6PD · Bull's-eye → thalassemia · Crescent → sickle · Fragment → microangiopathic · Howell-Jolly → no spleen.

The four inherited defects

  • HS — AD, cytoskeleton (ANK1/SPTB/SLC4A1). MCHC ↑, osmotic fragility ↑, Coombs NEGATIVE. Tx: folate, transfusion, splenectomy.
  • G6PD — X-linked; enzyme unstable, so old cells fail. Bite/blister cells, Heinz bodies (supravital stain). Triggers: infection (commonest), sulfa/antimalarials/nitrofurantoin, fava beans. Assay falsely normal during a crisis — repeat at 2–3 months. A− class III (mild) vs Mediterranean class II (severe).
  • Thalassemia — 4 alpha genes (chr 16), 2 beta (chr 11). The unpaired partner is the poison. HbH = β₄ (1 alpha gene); Hb Barts = γ₄ (0 alpha) = hydrops. Beta major presents at 3–6 months when the globin switch happens. Electrophoresis: ↑HbF, ↑HbA₂. Tx: transfusion + chelation.
  • Sickle — glutamic acid → valine, 6th codon, HBB. Deoxy-HbS polymerizes. Disease = HbS 90–100%, HbA absent; trait = 40–45% HbS + HbA.

Sickle — crises & management

  • Aplastic → retic LOW (parvovirus B19). Sequestration → retic high + huge spleen. Vaso-occlusive → Hgb near baseline; dactylitis in infants.
  • Worse with ↑HbS%, ↑concentration (dehydration), acidosis.
  • Hydroxyurea ↑HbF — boxed: myelosuppression + carcinogenic. Penicillin prophylaxis + pneumococcal/meningococcal/Hib. Folic acid. Chronic transfusion to HbS < 30% for stroke prevention. Casgevy (CRISPR, BCL11A) / Lyfgenia (lentiviral beta gene), age ≥12.
  • Fever in a sickle child = emergency (autosplenectomy).

Asplenia

  • Encapsulated organisms: S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis, H. influenzae b.
  • Prevent: vaccinate (before surgery if planned) · daily penicillin in children · any fever is an emergency.
Benign WBC · LO 4 · 66 · 76

Counts & the ANC

  • WBC 4.3–10.8 ×10⁹/L. Neutrophils 45–74% · lymphocytes 16–45% · monocytes 4–10% · eos 0–7% · baso 0–2%.
  • ANC = WBC × % neutrophils. <1,000 risk ↑ · <500 own flora · <200 no inflammatory response — fever may be the only sign.
  • Neutropenia: adults → drugs, children → infections; also destruction, sequestration, pooling.
  • Duffy null = constitutionally low ANC, no infection risk. Same antigen P. vivax uses.

Reactive vs malignant

  • Reactive: ↓N:C, cytoplasmic “skirt”, coarse chromatin, small nucleoli, varied, other lines normal.
  • Malignant: ↑N:C, scant cytoplasm, open chromatin, prominent nucleoli, monoclonal, cytopenias.
  • Blast pyramid: reactive keeps normal proportions; more blasts than anything = leukemia. Auer rod = malignant; a nucleolus alone is not.
  • Leukemoid: Döhle bodies, toxic granules, vacuoles + myelocytes + nRBC + tear-drops = leukoerythroblastic → myelofibrosis or a space-occupying lesion. Basophilia → think CML (BCR-ABL).

Immunodeficiencies

  • Shares: antibody 65% · combined 15% · phagocyte 10% · cellular 5% · complement 5%.
  • CGD: NADPH oxidase/cytochrome b558, 70% X-linked; catalase-positive organisms survive → granulomas. NBT test. IFN-γ ↓infection up to 70%.
  • Bruton: BTK, X-linked, well ~6 months (maternal IgG), no CD19/CD20, absent tonsils → monthly IgG.
  • DiGeorge: 22q11, CATCH-22, 3rd/4th branchial pouch → irradiated blood products, no live vaccines.
  • SCID: common γ chain or ADA; sick from month 2; TREC newborn screen, ALC <2,000 → transplant, earlier is better.
Bleeding · LO 32 · 50 · 68 · 79

Pattern → half of hemostasis

  • Mucocutaneous → VWD / platelets (primary) · joint + muscle → hemophilia (secondary) · delayed → factor 13 or antiplasmin.
  • Intrinsic 12→11→9→8 = aPTT · extrinsic 7 = PT · common 10→5→2→fibrinogen = both. Liver makes all except VWF and factor 8.
  • 50% rule: aPTT prolongs ~50%, 30–40% still fine, <20% = hemophilia (>45 s), <1% severe (>100 s).
  • Mix corrects → deficiency; fails → inhibitor. Incubate 30–60 min at 37 °C for a slow factor 8 antibody.
  • Blind spot: PT/aPTT are plasma assays — VWD and platelet defects are invisible. Citrate tube 9:1 or falsely prolonged.

The named disorders

  • Hemophilia A = factor 8 (1:5,000 M), B = factor 9 (1:30,000 M), both X-linked; C = factor 11, autosomal, mild. Factor 12 prolongs the aPTT and does not bleed.
  • Severity <1% severe · 1–5% moderate · >5–30% mild. Treat to 20–30%. Inhibitor → emicizumab (bridges 9a–10).
  • VWD: commonest inherited bleeding disorder, autosomal dominant. Activity ≈ antigen → quantitative (1, 3); activity ≪ antigen (<0.5–0.7) → type 2. DDAVP for type 1; concentrate for 2B and 3. Group O runs lower.
  • Handshake: collagen→VWF→GPIb→platelet. Bernard–Soulier = GPIb missing (↓ristocetin, big platelets) · Glanzmann = GPIIb/IIIa (all but ristocetin ↓; aspirin looks the same).
  • Acquired: liver (rebalanced, INR misleads) · DIC (↓plt, ↑PT/aPTT, ↓fibrinogen trend, ↑↑D-dimer, schistocytes → treat the cause) · vitamin K (2,7,9,10 + C/S; factor 5 normal = vitamin K, low = liver) · acquired inhibitor (older, new diathesis, mix fails).
Transfusion · LO 31 · 61 · 74

Products & thresholds

  • RBC 1–6 °C, 42 d · Platelets room temp, agitated, 5–7 d · Plasma frozen 1 yr · Cryo = fibrinogen.
  • TRICC: transfuse < 7 g/dL, one unit then reassess. Liberal arm did worse.
  • Platelets: 10k hypoproliferative · 20k lumbar puncture · 25k preterm neonate. Not for ITP/dengue (consumptive).
  • 30% factor activity is enough to clot — ocean vs teacup. INR 1.1 needs nothing.
  • Warfarin reversal: 4F-PCC + vitamin K, not plasma.
  • Citrate chelates calcium → hypocalcemia in massive transfusion.

Immunohematology

  • ABO antibodies are naturally occurring IgM from infant gut flora → fix complement → intravascular. Alloantibodies are IgG from transfusion/pregnancy → extravascular.
  • O = universal RBC donor; AB = universal RBC recipient. Inverts for plasma.
  • 15% RhD negative; O-negative ≈ 6%. Save D-negative for females of childbearing potential.
  • Alloimmunization 1–3% general, 20–60% in sickle cell disease.
  • Direct Coombs = on the cell (diagnosis). Indirect = in the plasma (the screen).
  • Screen = ready. Cross = reserved.

Reactions

  • Steps: stop → keep line open → clerical check → treat → send to blood bank.
  • Febrile non-hemolytic (cytokines; prevent by leukoreduction) · Septic (platelets, room temp) · Acute hemolytic ABO (back/flank pain, pink plasma, DAT+, “never event”, from wrong blood in tube) · Allergic / anaphylaxis (IgA deficiency, washed products).
  • TRALI: hypotensive, normal JVP, donor anti-HLA (14–20% of parous women), <6 h, diuresis no help. TACO: hypertensive, raised JVP, diuresis helps.
  • Transfusion GVHD: pancytopenia 1–6 wk, usually fatal. Prevent by irradiation — also for related donors.
  • Most common infectious risk = bacterially contaminated platelet.
Pharmacology · LO 8 · 9 · 12 · 26 · 62 · 69 · 70 · 71 · 72

Anticoagulants

  • UFH = long rope (IIa + Xa) · LMWH = short (mostly Xa) · fondaparinux = pentasaccharide (pure Xa, no HIT, no reversal).
  • Protamine: full → partial → none.
  • HIT: platelets fall 5–10 d, >50% drop; danger is thrombosis. Stop all heparin; give argatroban/bivalirudin/fondaparinux. Never warfarin alone (protein C → limb gangrene).
  • Warfarin: VKORC1 → factors II, VII, IX, X + protein C & S (“1972”). Factor VII shortest half-life → INR moves before the patient is anticoagulated → bridge ≥5 days. Teratogenic.
  • ↑INR: amiodarone, metronidazole, TMP/SMX, fluconazole, cipro, acute alcohol binge, gut-flora-killing antibiotics. ↓INR: rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's wort, chronic alcohol, leafy greens. Pharmacogenomics: CYP2C9 + VKORC1.
  • Xabans: Apixaban all-round safest · Rivaroxaban requires a meal · Edoxaban avoid if CrCl >95.
  • Reversal: protamine · vitamin K + 4F-PCC · idarucizumab (dabigatran) · 4F-PCC (xabans) · nothing (fondaparinux).

Antiplatelets & fibrinolytics

  • White clot (arterial) = antiplatelet. Red clot (venous) = anticoagulant.
  • Aspirin irreversibly acetylates COX-1 for the platelet's 7–10 day life; low dose is the antiplatelet dose.
  • Clopidogrel = prodrug, CYP2C19 (avoid omeprazole; use pantoprazole) · prasugrel contraindicated after stroke/TIA · ticagrelor reversible, dyspnea, aspirin ≤100 mg · cangrelor IV, minutes.
  • GP IIb/IIIa (eptifibatide, tirofiban): thrombocytopenia + worst bleeding. Vorapaxar: absolute contraindication with prior stroke/TIA/ICH. Cilostazol: boxed — contraindicated in heart failure.
  • -plase = plasminogen activator. Tenecteplase = single bolus, now approved for stroke. Catastrophic bleed → cryoprecipitate + tranexamic acid.

Anemia drugs

  • Oral iron: Fe²⁺ only, acidic duodenum; avoid enteric-coated/SR; alternate-day dosing; retic bump 7–10 d; continue 3–6 months past normalization.
  • IV iron: premedicate with methylprednisolone, NOT diphenhydramine. Ferric carboxymaltose → hypophosphatemia (FGF23) + heart failure indication.
  • Iron toxicity: acute → deferoxamine. Chronic overload → deferasirox (renal/hepatic/GI boxed), deferiprone (agranulocytosis), deferoxamine (8–12 h infusions, ocular/auditory).
  • ESAs: stimulate erythroid progenitor division/differentiation + retic release. IV or SC (SC needs 15–30% less). Approved: CKD + chemotherapy anemia. 4 boxed warnings, all at Hgb > 11 g/dL; cut the dose if it rises >1 g/dL in 2 weeks. HTN in up to ⅓. Replete iron first.
  • Leucovorin bypasses DHFR — the rescue for methotrexate. Luspatercept unblocks late maturation in thalassemia.
  • Complement inhibitors (PNH) → boxed meningococcal warning, vaccinate first.
Sepsis & HIV · LO 1 · 14 · 22 · 24 · 75 · 77

Sepsis

  • Dysregulated host response. Cultures negative in 30–50%. 1.7 M cases, ~350k deaths, $62 B/yr.
  • SOFA ≥ 2 ≈ 10% mortality. qSOFA: RR ≥22, SBP ≤100, altered mentation. NEWS2 = 6 params (adds O₂ sat); MEWS = 5, more sensitive. SIRS criteria are bidirectional.
  • Source order: respiratory > urinary > abdominal. 20% polymicrobial. Abdominal → B. fragilis.
  • G(−) → LPS · G(+) → lipoteichoic acid + peptidoglycan · fungal → β-glucans/mannans/chitin · viral → DAMPs.
  • Inflammation → endothelial dysfunction + ischemia → sequential organ failure → necrosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, autophagy.
  • Procalcitonin = the one cause-specific marker (bacterial). Bundle: bactericidal antibiotic, IV fluids, lactate, assess perfusion, vasopressors + source control.
  • Mortality 10–52%; post-sepsis syndrome in 50% of survivors; 90% survival if young/healthy.

HIV

  • pol → RT (high error rate), integrase, protease · gag → p24 (easiest to detect), p17, p6/7/9 · env → gp120 (binds CD4/CCR5/CXCR4), gp41 (fusion). gag/pol cleaved by viral protease; env by host protease.
  • Cycle → drug: attachment/fusion (fostemsavir gp120, maraviroc CCR5, enfuvirtide gp41, ibalizumab post-attachment) · RT (NRTI competitive/chain-terminating; NNRTI allosteric — one mutation kills the class; lenacapavir capsid) · integration (INSTI) · maturation (PI — resistance uncommon). Cobicistat is not an antiretroviral.
  • Testing: Ag/Ab combo with p24 → antibody differentiation → NAT (positive = acute). Eclipse period day 0–10 detects nothing. Western blot obsolete.
  • CD4 stages; viral load transmits. AIDS = CD4 <200 (or <14%) OR an AIDS-defining OI. OI risk greatest <50. ALC <1,000 predicts CD4 <200.
  • Thresholds: <200 PJP/Cryptococcus/Candida · <100 toxoplasmosis (ring-enhancing) · <50 CMV, Cryptococcus, MAC, CNS lymphoma.
  • PJP: cholesterol not ergosterol → TMP/SMX. Steroids if PaO₂ <70 or A–a ≥35. Start ART within 2 weeks.
  • IRIS: 10–20%, median 48 days, improving CD4/viral load with clinical worsening. Do not delay ART.
  • Prophylaxis: TMP/SMX <200 (PJP) and <100 (toxo). Live vaccines only if CD4 ≥ 200.
  • Boosters (ritonavir, cobicistat) inhibit CYP3A4 → OTC nasal steroid → iatrogenic Cushing.
  • U = U, CDC < 200 copies/mL. PARTNER trial: 74,000 episodes, zero transmissions. PrEP ~99% sexual; PEP within 72 hours.
Clotting · LO 33 · 43 · 44 · 45 · 46 · 48 · 52

Microangiopathies

  • All TMAs: PT, aPTT and fibrinogen normal. DIC: both prolonged, fibrinogen falling. Schistocytes in all — the panel is the discriminator.
  • TTP = ADAMTS13 deficient or IgG-inhibited → uncleaved ultra-large VWF multimers seize platelets → systemic, brain. Acquired form presents in the 20s–30s. Rx plasma exchange (replaces enzyme + removes antibody); no platelet transfusion. Pentad: fever, ↓plt, MAHA, neuro, renal — treat before all five.
  • HUS = Shiga toxin (E. coli O157:H7) → glomerular endothelium → kidney, bloody diarrhoea prodrome.
  • aHUS = unregulated alternative complement (factor H, factor I, MCP/CD46, thrombomodulin) → MAC destroys endothelium. Rx eculizumab. Genotype before transplant — liver-made regulator recurs, kidney-made one does not.

Thrombophilias

  • Virchow: endothelial damage · stasis · hypercoagulability. ~25% of everyone has a thrombophilia; ~60% of DVT patients do.
  • Lose a brake (antithrombin, protein C, protein S) → RR 15–19×. Push the accelerator (factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A) → RR 3–5×.
  • FVL R506Q, 5% of Caucasians, 20–30% of VTE — activated protein C resistance. G20210A is a 3′ UTR mutation raising mRNA stability: normal protein, more of it.
  • Antithrombin: homozygous lethal; lost in proteinuria, chest tubes → heparin resistance. Homozygous protein C/S → neonatal purpura fulminans.
  • Lupus anticoagulant = double misnomer. Mix fails to correct; extra phospholipid corrects. Anti-cardiolipin → false-positive syphilis test. Needs a clinical criterion plus persistence 12 weeks apart. Antibody without the syndrome is not anticoagulated.

HIT & duration

  • HIT is prothrombotic. Days 5–14, >50% fall, venous:arterial 4:1. Anti-heparin/PF4 antibody, then SRA if it conflicts.
  • 4T: 6–8 treat, don't wait · 0–3 do not test · 4–5 test. Rx argatroban / lepirudin. Avoid: warfarin alone (gangrene), LMWH (cross-reacts), no anticoagulation.
  • Only a long heparin tail bridges antithrombin to thrombin → aPTT works for UFH (1.5–2.5×) but anti-Xa only for LMWH/fondaparinux.
  • Recurrence sets duration: major transient 3% · minor reversible 5% · unprovoked/persistent 10% in year one.
  • Test if: family history, <45 yrs, no risk factors, recurrent, unusual site. Heparin ↓antithrombin, warfarin ↓protein C/S, acute illness ↑factor 8 → wait months, off anticoagulation. Genetic testing any time, once.
Malignancy · LO 3 · 6 · 21 · 37 · 38 · 39 · 40 · 41 · 54 · 56 · 57 · 58 · 66 · 67 · 80 · 82

The core construct

  • Less differentiated → AGGRESSIVE. More differentiated → INDOLENT. Tempo follows the stage at transformation.
  • >20% blasts = acute leukemia (marrow OR blood). Same line: MDS vs AML, and CML blast phase.
  • Aggressive lymphoma is CURABLE; indolent is not — chemo kills dividing cells. A third of follicular patients are never treated.
  • Emergencies: DIC (low fibrinogen, long PT/PTT, schistocytes — APL) and tumour lysis (K, PO4, urate UP, Ca DOWN).

Myeloid

  • APL: t(15;17) → PML-RARA → ATRA on suspicion. DIC kills before cytogenetics return. 90% survival.
  • MDS: hypercellular marrow + cytopenias = ineffective production. Epigenetic → hypomethylating agents. 30% → AML, but 50% die of bleeding/infection.
  • CML: t(9;22) / BCR-ABL / Philadelphia. TKI → median 2–5 yr becomes 95% alive at 10 yr. T315I → ponatinib.
  • Accelerated = blasts 10–19% OR basophils ≥20%. Blast phase = >20% blasts. Read the cell type before the number.
  • CML vs leukemoid: BCR-ABL positive, basophilia, LOW leukocyte alkaline phosphatase (leukemoid = high LAP, toxic granulation, a trigger).
  • MPNs are JAK2-driven — danger is thrombosis. PV → phlebotomy (Hct <45 M / <42 F). ET → hydroxyurea. MF → pancytopenia; ruxolitinib helps symptoms + survival, NOT counts.

Lymphoid & plasma cell

  • ALL: commonest childhood cancer; lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, CNS distinguish it from AML. Children >90% → adults 30%.
  • CLL: CD5+, CD10−, CD20+, CD23+. vs mantle cell: CD23 NEGATIVE + t(11;14)/cyclin D1. 13q favourable; 17p/11q high risk; IgVH mutated = good.
  • DLBCL → R-CHOP. IPI 4–5 = 50%; double hit (MYC + BCL2/BCL6) ≈ 30% — worse than the worst clinical score. Burkitt t(8;14).
  • Gastric MALT → eradicate H. pylori and the lymphoma can resolve. Hepatitis C → nodal MZL.
  • Hodgkin: bimodal 15–35 / >55; 50% EBV; EXCISIONAL biopsy; stage with PET/CT + ESR; alcohol-induced nodal pain, pruritus, eosinophilia. ABVD.
  • Waldenström = IgM pentamer → hyperviscosity (emergent plasmapheresis), neuropathy, cold agglutinins, cryoglobulins, bleeding. MYD88.
  • Myeloma: damage from the product. Light chains → renal failure even with a small spike. CRAB. MGUS <10% & <3 g/dL (1%/yr); smoldering 10–60% or ≥3 g/dL (10%/yr for 5 yr). Neither is treated.
Antineoplastics · LO 11 · 15 · 16 · 17 · 64

Principles

  • Four classes: cytotoxic (dividing cells) · targeted (follows the target) · immuno (autoimmunity) · hormonal (hormone withdrawal).
  • Neoadjuvant = before surgery; adjuvant = after. Log kill = constant FRACTION, so cycles are mathematically necessary. 10⁹ cells ≈ 1 g = detection threshold.
  • Combine drugs with different mechanisms AND different organ toxicity — shared toxicity forces dose reduction.
  • Rescue: leucovorin↔methotrexate (but ENHANCES 5-FU) · mesna↔acrolein · dexrazoxane↔anthracycline.
  • Resistance: DNA repair (alkylators/cisplatin) · trapping (bleomycin, cisplatin, anthracyclines) · altered DHFR (methotrexate) · failed prodrug activation (antimetabolites) · MDR1/P-glycoprotein efflux → cross-resistance to unrelated drugs · sanctuary (CNS, testes).
  • G0 cells resist CCS drugs — which is why CCNS alkylators/platinums/anthracyclines are the backbone.

Chemo Man — the six pairings

  • Cyclophosphamide/ifosfamide → haemorrhagic cystitis (acrolein; mesna)
  • Bleomycin → pulmonary fibrosis (marrow SPARED)
  • Cisplatin → oto- + nephrotoxicity + vomiting; carboplatin trades these for myelosuppression; oxaliplatin → COLD-induced neuropathy
  • Doxorubicin/daunorubicin/trastuzumab → cardiotoxicity (lifetime cumulative cap)
  • Oxaliplatin/vincristine/taxanes → peripheral neuropathy
  • Irinotecan → potentially fatal diarrhoea (atropine early, loperamide late)
  • Also: vincristine NEVER intrathecal (fatal). vinCRIStine → nerves; vinBLAStine → marrow. Cytarabine → cerebellar ataxia + conjunctivitis. Procarbazine is an MAO inhibitor.
  • Support: epoetin (RBC) · filgrastim (neutrophils) · sargramostim · plerixafor (mobilisation) · -opag/-ostim (platelets). -setron = 5-HT3; -pitant = NK1.

Targeted, immuno & hormonal

  • Size decides site: antibodies (~145 kDa) act outside the cell; small molecules go inside. No mAbs among signal-transduction inhibitors.
  • Small molecule → CYP3A4 interactions, food effects, QT prolongation, diarrhoea, raised LFTs. Antibody → infusion reactions + on-target toxicity (EGFR → acne-like rash).
  • Trastuzumab → heart failure (HER2 on cardiomyocytes). Daratumumab (CD38) interferes with cross-match — warn the blood bank.
  • ADC: the second word is the payload. -emtansine/-vedotin = anti-microtubule (neuropathy); -deruxtecan/-govitecan = topoisomerase (myelosuppression).
  • Checkpoints: CTLA-4 = ipilimumab · PD-1 = pembrolizumab/nivolumab · PD-L1 = atezolizumab, avelumab, durvalumab · LAG-3 = relatlimab. Toxicity = any organ ending in -itis → treat with corticosteroids.
  • CRS (4–16 h, fever + hypotension + hypoxia) → tocilizumab. ICANS (day 3–9, confusion/seizure) → dexamethasone, NOT tocilizumab.
  • Hormonal: SERM = agonist in bone and endometrium (tamoxifen → endometrial cancer + clots, protects bone). Aromatase inhibitor removes estrogen everywhere → osteoporosis; postmenopausal only. GnRH agonist → tumour FLARE → cover with an anti-androgen. Abiraterone (CYP17) → hypokalaemia, give with prednisone.
Lymphatics & VTE · LO 10 · 43 · 63

Lymphatics

  • Primary function = return proteins and fluid to the blood. Lymph is protein-rich — which is why obstruction swells so persistently.
  • NEVER place a LEFT internal jugular line — the thoracic duct enters the posterior wall at the left subclavian/IJ confluence → chylothorax. Use right IJ, subclavian or femoral.
  • Node: cortex = B cells/follicles · paracortex = T cells · medulla = plasma cells. Antigen-INdependent maturation in marrow/thymus; antigen-DEPENDENT in nodes/spleen/mucosa.
  • Thymus: cortex → positive selection → medulla; non-responders apoptose. Involutes after puberty.
  • Peyer's patches (ileum) → IgA = “mucosal antiseptic paint”; tonsils → IgG/IgE, directly antigen-exposed and non-encapsulated. Ileocecal resection in a baby → loses IgA → translocation sepsis.
  • Lymphedema: square toes = Stemmer sign (cannot tent the web space). Unilateral + negative ultrasound + square toes. Developed world = iatrogenic; worldwide = filariasis (Wuchereria bancrofti). Complication: lymphangiosarcoma.

DVT/PE — starting and stopping

  • DVT → Doppler ultrasound first, always. CTEPH → V/Q scan, not CT angiography.
  • Apixaban and Rivaroxaban go it alone (loading dose, no heparin). Dabigatran and Edoxaban need Days of heparin first.
  • DOAC preferred over warfarin — equal efficacy, LESS bleeding (especially intracranial). Except: pregnancy · massive clot · extremes of weight · gastric bypass · CrCl <30 · HIT · splanchnic · antiphospholipid syndrome (evidence of HARM) · enzyme inducers.
  • Duration: provoked = 3 months, then stop. Unprovoked = at least 3 months then decide. Second unprovoked = long-term. Cancer = LMWH ≥3 months.
  • Fatal recurrence 0.3–1%/yr vs fatal bleeding 0.2–0.6%/yr — overlapping, hence “suggest” and Grade 2B.
  • AMPLIFY-EXTEND: apixaban 2.5 mg = full benefit, placebo-level bleeding. EINSTEIN-CHOICE: aspirin is markedly inferior and not safer.
  • VTE-BLEED ≥2 = high risk (13%); active cancer alone scores 2. IVC filter: one solid indication — absolute contraindication to anticoagulation; it increases recurrent DVT.
Blood & lymph infections · LO 5 · 13 · 22 · 23

Viral hemorrhagic fevers — one mechanism

  • A immunosuppression (APCs immature: low co-stimulation, low type I IFN) → B cytokine storm → C infected phagocytes disseminate to brain, adrenal, liver, spleen → D capillary leak, DIC, MODS. This is sepsis by a viral route.
  • Patient bleeds for three reasons: consumption (storm) + failure of production (liver) + leaky vessel.
  • Acetaminophen, NEVER NSAIDs — class rule for every agent. No antiviral except Lassa.
  • All five families: enveloped, ssRNA, animal/insect reservoir. Bunyavirales 3 segments · Arenaviridae 2 · Flaviviridae non-segmented.

The four agents — discriminators only

  • Dengue — reservoir is HUMANS; Aedes aegypti (prefers humans, thins ~New Jersey) > albopictus. Extrinsic 8–10 d, intrinsic 4–7 d. Retro-orbital + breakbone pain. Tier2 = tier1 + any TWO; tier3 = + any ONE severe. Dengvaxia only if PRIOR dengue.
  • Hantavirus HFRS — rodent aerosol, NO vector, incubation 3–9 WEEKS. AKI is the cause of death (immunopathologic tubulointerstitial nephritis). Old World → kidney; New World → HCPS (lungs). S=Surrounds RNA, M=Membrane spikes (antibody target), L=Largest (RdRp).
  • CCHF — Hyalomma tick, livestock/birds stay well, SE Europe/Turkey. ~88% subclinical, ~30% mortality in the symptomatic. Not Ixodes.
  • Lassa — Mastomys natalensis, West Africa, no vector (contact, aerosol, or eating the rodent). Bilateral sensorineural hearing loss in survivors. Only drug: ribavirin (weak).

Six organisms, one grid

  • Bat→filovirus · cat→Bartonella · rat→plague · mouse→Babesia · human→malaria. No vector→filovirus · cat flea · rat flea · deer tick NYMPH · Anopheles.
  • Ebola: no vector; virus persists in a CORPSE; wide host-cell range. Zaire-directed Inmazeb/Ebanga/Ervebo fail against Bundibugyo — strain mismatch. Marburg: CAVES, more hemorrhage, 50% (88–90% severe), nothing approved.
  • Bartonella henselae: inoculum is FLEA FECES, claw is the needle. Immunocompetent child → cat scratch disease (papule → tender regional node; azithromycin; do NOT culture; Warthin–Starry). Immunocompromised → bacillary angiomatosis (cherry-red papules, mimics Kaposi; biopsy + PCR, serology unreliable; erythromycin/doxycycline + ART). Coccobacilli: Bartonella, Haemophilus, Bordetella. Argyrophilic: Bartonella, spirochetes, Legionella, H. pylori.
  • Plague: bipolar “safety pin” G(−) rod; BUBO; acral gangrene = “Black Death”; western US, ≥17 states. Blocked proventriculus makes a starving flea bite frantically. Humans are a DEAD-END host. Bubonic 1–15%/50%; pneumonic & septicemic 50%/100%. Gentamicin or a quinolone; rapid antigen 100%/100%. High resistance → suspect bioengineering.
  • Babesia microti: white-footed mouse (also Lyme, anaplasma, Powassan, hanta) + Ixodes NYMPH (ticks not born infected; nymph is invisible → no tick-bite history expected). Rings on Giemsa, hemolytic anemia + thrombocytopenia. Azithromycin + atovaquone (atovaquone hits electron transport). Doxycycline does NOT treat it.
  • Malaria: liver stage first (Babesia has none). Fever interval = the erythrocytic cycle: knowlesi 24 h · vivax/ovale 48 h · malariae 72 h · falciparum IRREGULAR (unsynchronized → most dangerous). THICK = is it there; THIN = which one. Chloroquine blocks heme polymerase → free heme poisons the parasite; resistant → ACT. RTS,S/AS01 targets the SPOROZOITE; hepatitis B protein is only a carrier.
Porphyria & lead · LO 35

Heme synthesis gone wrong

  • Heme inhibits ALAS1. A downstream block → less heme → less inhibition → ALAS1 revs up → precursors above the block flood out. Disease of precursor accumulation, not heme deficiency.
  • Precipitants all raise ALAS1: fasting/low carbohydrate (PGC-1α), P450-inducing drugs, sex hormones, alcohol, infection, stress. Glucose and insulin suppress PGC-1α → carbohydrate loading; hemin restores feedback.
  • AIP — PBG deaminase, autosomal dominant, partial (half) deficiency so onset 15–50, women > men. Triad: visceral abdominal pain with NORMAL imaging + neurologic dysfunction + psychiatric disturbance, plus hypertension, tachycardia, hyponatremia, urine that darkens on standing. SPOT urine during the attack for ALA, PBG and creatinine; > 2–4× ULN confirms.
  • PCT — the most common porphyria overall; UROD. Blistering photosensitivity + milia + hypertrichosis of cheeks/temples + TEA-COLOURED urine. Associations: hepatitis C, alcohol, iron overload, ESRD; hemochromatosis mutations in two-thirds. Treat: DAA, phlebotomy, hydroxychloroquine, sun avoidance.
  • THE DISCRIMINATOR: lead blocks ALA dehydratase (above PBG) → ↑ALA with NORMAL PBG. AIP blocks PBG deaminase (below PBG) → ↑ALA AND ↑PBG. Lead also inhibits ferrochelatase → ZPP (3-month screen); microcytic anemia with basophilic stippling, Burton's lines, wrist/foot drop, saturnine gout.
Lymph node pathology · LO 63 · 66

Normal node & the reactive mimics

  • Blue vs pink at low power IS the pattern, and the pattern generates the differential. Cortex = B/follicles · paracortex = T · medulla = cords and sinuses. Subcapsular sinus = where metastasis lands first.
  • Reactive germinal center: polarized + tingible body macrophages + intact mantle + spaced follicles + BCL2 NEGATIVE. Normal GC B cells switch BCL2 off so failed clones can die — t(14;18) forces it back on = follicular lymphoma.
  • Formalin is a one-way door — it kills flow cytometry and karyotype. IHC, FISH, PCR/NGS still work on paraffin.
  • Site: anterior cervical→mono · posterior cervical→toxoplasmosis/Kikuchi · hard fixed supraclavicular→malignant until proven otherwise · mediastinal→nodular sclerosis Hodgkin.
  • Infectious mononucleosis vs Hodgkin — the single most testable discrimination. RS-LIKE cells RETAIN the B-cell program (CD20 +, OCT2 +, PAX5 normal); RS cells have LOST it. CD30 and EBV are shared and therefore useless here.
  • Kikuchi–Fujimoto: young Asian woman, tender cervical node, antibiotic-unresponsive. Necrosis with NO neutrophils + crescentic histiocytes CD68 + MPO +. Send lupus serology; treatment is supportive.
  • HLH: 5 of 8 criteria — fever, splenomegaly, cytopenias, high ferritin, high triglycerides, LOW fibrinogen, hemophagocytic histiocytes. Hemophagocytosis = debris being destroyed; EMPERIPOLESIS = intact cells passing through (Rosai–Dorfman).
  • Castleman (hyaline vascular): atretic dendritic-cell follicles + onion-skinning + lollipop vessel + twinning; risk of follicular dendritic cell sarcoma. Rosai–Dorfman: massive sinus expansion, S100 + (negative in the engulfed cells) · CD1a − · CD207 −; MAP kinase mutations → MEK inhibitors.
Lymphomas in depth · LO 21 · 41 · 56 · 57

Two questions, then the phenotype

  • CD5 + → CLL/SLL or mantle cell (separate on CD23: CLL POSITIVE, mantle NEGATIVE + t(11;14) cyclin D1). CD10 + → germinal center: follicular, Burkitt, a subset of DLBCL. CD5 − and CD10 − → marginal zone, lymphoplasmacytic, hairy cell.
  • Hodgkin = orderly CONTIGUOUS spread down axial groups; abdominal nodes are never involved before cervical/mediastinal. Non-Hodgkin = noncontiguous, peripheral, mesenteric/Waldeyer, extranodal.
  • Classic Hodgkin: RS cells are <10% (often 1–2%) of the node. CD30 strong · CD15 ~75% · PAX5 WEAK vs background B cells · MUM1 + · CD20/OCT2/BOB1/CD45/ALK NEGATIVE. ABVD; relapse → brentuximab (anti-CD30) or nivolumab.
  • Subtypes: nodular sclerosis (young, female-leaning, early stage, collagen bands + lacunar cells, excellent) · lymphocyte-rich (good) · mixed cellularity (elderly male, high stage, NO bands, aggressive) · lymphocyte-depleted (elderly male, HIV, disseminated, worst; most EBV-associated).
  • NLPHL is the mirror image: POPCORN cell (multilobated, vesicular, irregular nucleolus), CD20/OCT2/BOB1/CD45 POSITIVE, CD30 and CD15 NEGATIVE, PAX5 STRONG, rosetted by CD4+CD57+PD-1+ T-follicular helper cells. B symptoms uncommon; R-CHOP works because CD20 is there.

The mature lymphomas, one line each

  • CLL/SLL: smudge cells + soccer-ball chromatin; proliferation centers (pseudofollicles) in the node. TP53 (17p) is always bad. Richter transformation → DLBCL, driven by new TP53 or MYC.
  • Follicular: BCL2 POSITIVE, t(14;18), CD10 +, BCL6 +; back-to-back monotonous follicles, no tingible body macrophages, PARATRABECULAR marrow. Indolent, incurable; transforms to DLBCL 1–3%/yr. Graded on centroblast count.
  • Burkitt: BCL2 NEGATIVE, t(8;14) MYC, Ki-67 > 95%, STARRY SKY, medium cells with vacuoles. Endemic = EBV, jaw; sporadic = abdomen/omentum, can cause volvulus; immunodeficiency = HIV, nodal. Grow-fast vs die-slow — the opposite of follicular.
  • MALT/EMZL: monocytoid cells + LYMPHOEPITHELIAL LESIONS; CD5 −, CD10 −, BCL6 − (marginal zone is outside the germinal center). H. pylori eradication alone can cure it.
  • Hairy cell: middle-aged male, DRY TAP from marrow fibrosis, MONOCYTOPENIA, pancytopenia + splenomegaly yet indolent. BRAF > 90%; CD11c/CD25/CD103/annexin A1.
  • Mycosis fungoides: cerebriform haloed lymphocytes, EPIDERMOTROPISM, PAUTRIER microabscesses, “bathing trunk” distribution; CD4 +, CD8 −, CD7 dim/lost. Sézary = erythroderma + >1,000 cells/µL + CD4:CD8 > 10:1 + marker loss + same TCR clone in blood and skin. Dermatopathic node is REACTIVE — calling it involved upstages the patient wrongly.
  • ALCL: HALLMARK cells (horseshoe nucleus, pale paranuclear area); CD30 strong and diffuse (the other CD30 lymphoma), often CD3 −, EMA +, CD45 +. ALK + ~80% survival vs ALK − ~50%. Sinusoidal growth mimics carcinoma/melanoma. Breast implant-associated ALCL: 100% 5-year survival.
  • ATLL: Japan or the Caribbean → HTLV-1; FLOWER cells (multilobated petals) — not the cerebriform single folded mass of a Sézary cell. Hypercalcemia.
Pediatrics, spleen & prescribing · LO 65 · 67 · 77 · 78

ITP — two diseases, one name

  • IgG against GP2b/3a; antibody MADE in the spleen and platelets EATEN in the spleen — which is why splenectomy fixes both ends. MCC of thrombocytopenia in adults and children.
  • Acute (<1 yr): child, post-viral, 80% resolve spontaneously → observe (ICH <0.1%). Chronic (>1 yr): adult women, often 2° to SLE/RA/thyroid/CLL.
  • Labs: ISOLATED profound thrombocytopenia, normal PT/PTT, no schistocytes, marrow megakaryocytes INCREASED. Splenomegaly is RARE — a big spleen sends you elsewhere.
  • Wet purpura (oral blood blisters) = higher bleed risk. Steroids give a sustained response in only ~20%; IVIG is a short-lived bridge; splenectomy 75–85%.
  • Accessory spleens in 20% of people, >80% near the hilum — miss one and ITP recurs.

The pancytopenic child

  • WELL-appearing + no organomegaly + ACELLULAR marrow = aplastic anemia. SICK-appearing + splenomegaly + packed marrow = leukemia. That split comes before any test.
  • Fanconi: short stature, café-au-lait, ABSENT thumbs; confirm with chromosome breakage (diepoxybutane). TAR: absent radii but thumbs PRESENT. Diamond-Blackfan: pure red cell aplasia, normal WBC/platelets. Shwachman-Diamond: pancreatic insufficiency + neutropenia. Wiskott-Aldrich: X-linked, eczema, infections, SMALL platelets.
  • Splenic sequestration: infants <2 with HbSS; Hb falls >2 g/dL below baseline, fatal within hours. Fever >38.5 °C in sickle cell = EMERGENCY — cultures + broad-spectrum antibiotics now.
  • Hydroxyurea raises HbF — start in infancy for everyone. DTIs (bivalirudin, argatroban, dabigatran) are NOT approved in children; DOACs are fine.

A complete prescription (no lecture source)

Trauma, edema & metastasis · LO 34 · 47 · 60 · 73

Splenic injury — stability decides, not grade

  • Most commonly injured solid organ in blunt abdominal trauma. Left lower rib fractures are the warning. Kehr sign = referred LEFT shoulder pain from blood on the left hemidiaphragm (phrenic, C3–5), worse lying flat.
  • UNSTABLE → FAST → operating room. Never send an unstable trauma patient to CT. STABLE → CT with contrast, which grades it and finds the blush.
  • Three rungs: 1 medical — observation, bed rest, serial Hct/exam (the default, and near-universal in children); 2 interventional — angioembolization for a blush, pseudoaneurysm or grade IV–V; 3 surgical — splenorrhaphy/partial if stable, total splenectomy if unstable.
  • AAST: I–III track depth (<1 cm / 1–3 cm / >3 cm); IV–V involve the vessels (>25% devascularised, shattered, or hilar).
  • Delayed rupture classically day 5–6. Complications: pseudoaneurysm, abscess/infarct, pancreatic tail leak, gastric necrosis from short gastrics, splenic/portal vein thrombosis, subphrenic abscess + left pleural effusion, platelets >1 million → aspirin, splenosis, OPSI.
  • Vaccinate 2 weeks BEFORE an elective splenectomy; BEFORE DISCHARGE after a trauma splenectomy.

Edema after trauma — read the clock

  • Four Starling failures: ↑hydrostatic, ↓oncotic, ↑permeability, ↓lymphatic.
  • Hours → inflammation (exudate), haematoma, compartment syndrome. Days → DVT, hypoalbuminaemia/dilution. Weeks → lymphedema.
  • Compartment syndrome: pain out of proportion + pain on PASSIVE STRETCH are the early signs. Pulses stay palpable — it is a capillary problem, not an arterial one; pulselessness/pallor/paralysis are LATE. ΔP <30 mm Hg → fasciotomy. Then watch for rhabdomyolysis → ↑CK, hyperkalaemia, AKI.
  • Swelling that was improving and starts worsening again days later = DVT until imaged.
  • Texture: hard + agonising = compartment; pitting, one leg = DVT; pitting, both legs + sacrum = oncotic; non-pitting, square toes = lymphatic.

Lymphatic spread of carcinoma

  • Carcinoma spreads by lymphatics; sarcoma by blood. Lymphatic capillaries have no basement membrane and loose junctions — that is why.
  • Route: basement membrane → lymphovascular invasion → afferent lymphatic → SUBCAPSULAR (marginal) SINUS — where metastasis is seen FIRST and where the pathologist looks first → efferent → next echelon → thoracic duct → left subclavian vein, at which point it becomes haematogenous.
  • Sentinel node = first node draining the tumour; if negative, spare the dissection (and spare the secondary lymphedema it causes). Skip metastases happen when tumour obstructs lymphatics.
  • Virchow node = LEFT supraclavicular (Troisier sign) because the thoracic duct drains there → abdominal/gastric primary. Right supraclavicular → lung, oesophagus, mediastinum, head & neck.
  • Testis → para-aortic, NOT inguinal (embryologic descent); scrotal SKIN → inguinal. Anal canal: above the dentate line → internal iliac; below → superficial inguinal. Breast → axillary levels I–III by pectoralis minor.
  • Hard, fixed, painless, matted = metastatic; fixation means extranodal extension. Thoracic duct involvement → chylous effusion.
