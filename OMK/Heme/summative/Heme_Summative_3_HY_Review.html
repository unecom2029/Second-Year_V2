The four inherited defects ★ HY

  • Hereditary spherocytosis — autosomal dominant, cytoskeleton (ankyrin/spectrin/band 3). High concentration index, increased osmotic fragility, Coombs NEGATIVE (splits it from autoimmune). Folate, transfusion, splenectomy — defer past age 4.
  • G6PD — X-linked; enzyme unstable so old cells fail first. Bite/blister cells, Heinz bodies (supravital stain). Triggers: infection (commonest), sulfa, antimalarials, nitrofurantoin, fava beans. Assay is falsely NORMAL during a crisis — repeat at 2–3 months.
  • Thalassemia — 4 alpha genes (chr 16), 2 beta (chr 11). The unpaired partner chain is the poison. Hemoglobin H = β₄ (1 alpha left); Barts = γ₄ (0 alpha) = hydrops. Beta major presents at 3–6 months (globin switch). Electrophoresis ↑F, ↑A2. Transfusion + chelation.
  • Sickle — glutamic acid → valine, 6th codon; charged swapped for hydrophobic = sticky patch; deoxygenated hemoglobin S polymerizes. Disease = 90–100% S, no A. Trait = 40–45% S plus A.
🩺 Sickle — the reticulocyte tells you the crisis

Aplastic → retic LOW (parvovirus B19). Sequestration → retic HIGH + huge spleen; infants <2, hemoglobin falls >2 g/dL, fatal in hours. Vaso-occlusive → hemoglobin near baseline; dactylitis in infants.

Fever >38.5 °C in a sickle child = EMERGENCY (functional asplenia) — cultures + broad-spectrum antibiotics now.

Hydroxyurea raises fetal hemoglobin (≥20% reduces crises), 15 mg/kg, start in infancy; boxed myelosuppression + carcinogenicity. Penicillin prophylaxis, vaccines, folate. Chronic transfusion to HbS <30% for stroke prevention. Gene therapy: Casgevy (CRISPR/BCL11A), Lyfgenia — age ≥12.

  • Asplenia: encapsulated organisms — pneumococcus, meningococcus, Haemophilus influenzae b. Vaccinate 2 weeks before an elective splenectomy, before discharge after trauma. Most episodes within 2 years; highest risk if before age 4. Any fever → penicillin immediately.

Bite cells — the spleen removed a Heinz body.

Howell-Jolly = no working spleen.

Q: Hemolysis is established. Which finding proves it is happening inside the vessels?

Answer

Urinary hemosiderin. Retics, lactate dehydrogenase and unconjugated bilirubin are shared; spherocytes and splenomegaly point extravascular.

Products, Compatibility and Reactions

  • Storage: red cells 1–6 °C / 42 d · platelets room temperature, agitated, 5–7 d · plasma frozen 1 yr · cryoprecipitate = fibrinogen (+ factor 8, 13, von Willebrand).
  • TRICC: transfuse below 7 g/dL, one unit then reassess. The liberal arm did worse.
  • Platelet thresholds: 10k hypoproliferative · 20k lumbar puncture · 25k preterm neonate. Not for consumptive disease — immune thrombocytopenia, TTP, dengue.
  • 30% factor activity is enough to clot — an INR of 1.1 needs nothing. Warfarin reversal = 4-factor concentrate + vitamin K, not plasma.
  • Citrate chelates calcium → hypocalcemia in massive transfusion.

Blood groups

  • ABO antibodies = natural IgM → fix complement → intravascular. Alloantibodies (transfusion/pregnancy) = IgG → extravascular.
  • O = universal red cell donor; AB = universal recipient. Inverts for plasma. 15% RhD-negative; O-negative only ~6% — save it for females of childbearing potential.
  • Alloimmunization 1–3% general, 20–60% in sickle cell disease.
  • Direct Coombs = antibody ON the cell (diagnosis). Indirect = IN the plasma (the screen). Screen = ready; crossmatch = reserved.

Reactions ★ HY

Always: stop → keep the line open → clerical check → treat → return to blood bank.

☐ Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia — a CLOTTING emergency

Antibody against the heparin–platelet factor 4 complex activates platelets. Platelets fall days 5–14 by more than 50%; venous to arterial events about 4 to 1.
The platelet count drops and the patient CLOTS — every intuition here is backwards.
4T score: 6–8 treat now · 4–5 test · 0–3 do not test.
Do: stop all heparin → argatroban, bivalirudin or fondaparinux.
Do NOT: give warfarin alone (protein C falls first → limb gangrene) · switch to low-molecular-weight heparin (cross-reacts) · simply stop and give nothing.

The American Geriatrics Society adds a fifth: Multicomplexity.

🧠 The escalation logic — the most testable slide

Brief screen → positive → longer instrument → positive → intervention — and note where the intervention is not a drug.

  • Cognition: Mini-Cog → MoCA/SLUMS/MMSE → neuropsychiatric testing
  • Mood: PHQ-2 → PHQ-9 or Geriatric Depression Scale → social work and therapy FIRST, then medication
  • Sleep: sleep quality scale → sleep hygiene first
  • Falls/activity: Timed Up and Go, 4-Stage Balance (STEADI), RAPA → physical therapy
  • Function: Katz (basic) / Lawton–Brody (household) → occupational therapy
  • Frailty: Fried / Clinical Frailty Scale · Substances: CAGE-AID · Medications: Beers Criteria

You do NOT administer every tool — the skill examined is choosing the right one for the complaint you just heard.

  • Physical and occupational therapists predict successful discharge home; physicians and social workers do not. Function predicts outcome better than diagnosis.
  • Age-related hematologic change: marrow cellularity = (100 − age)%; monoclonal gammopathy, myelodysplasia and CLL all rise with age.
  • "Inflammaging" drives cardiovascular, bone and cognitive disease in treated HIV despite an undetectable load — monitor lipids, bone density, renal function, cognition.

Q: 82-year-old in a skilled nursing facility after delirium; previously lived alone. Whose assessment best predicts successful return home?

Answer

The physical and occupational therapists. They predicted successful discharge in a longitudinal cohort; medical providers and social workers did not.

🎯 Hematology Summative 3 — High-Yield One-Pager

All 18 topic areas, high-yield only. Red = a point to read twice.

1 · HEMATOPOIESIS & MARROW

Sites & hierarchy

  • Yolk sac → fetal liver & spleen → marrow. Adult = axial.
  • Stem cell = self-renew + differentiate. Too much self-renewal = leukemia.
  • Platelet is anucleate → aspirin lasts its 7–10 day life.
  • Erythropoietin from PERITUBULAR INTERSTITIAL cells of the renal cortex — beside the tubule, not inside. Proximal tubule = vitamin D; juxtaglomerular = renin.
  • CXCR4–CXCL12 tethers stem cells; plerixafor cuts it. Selectins = rolling, wrong compartment.

Marrow & the blood count

  • Prone, posterior superior iliac spine. Aspirate = cytology; core = architecture.
  • Cellularity ≈ 100 − age. Fat REPLACES marrow with age. M:E ratio 3:1.
  • Buffy coat = white cells + platelets. Dissolved protein cannot band.
  • Hematocrit ≈ 3 × hemoglobin, only if size is normal.
  • Reticulocytes LOW = production problem; HIGH = loss/destruction.
2 · ANEMIA I

Iron & inflammation

  • Iron deficiency: Fe ↓, ferritin ↓, TIBC ↑, saturation <15%, marrow iron absent.
  • Adult male / postmenopausal female = GI blood loss until proven otherwise → scope BOTH ends. One negative stool test proves nothing.
  • Hepcidin ↑ destroys ferroportin — doorman locks the exit. Ferroportin is the DOOR; more of it would help.
  • Inflammation row: iron ↓, ferritin ↑, TIBC ↓, marrow iron ↑. Ferritin and TIBC move OPPOSITE to deficiency.
  • Prussian blue = iron; blue ring = ringed sideroblast (mitochondria).
  • Thalassemia trait = MANY small cells, normal ferritin, narrow RDW. Iron deficiency = FEW small cells.

B12, folate & porphyria

  • B12 = DNA + myelin. Folate = DNA only. B12 has the Brain.
  • Methylmalonic acid ↑ in B12, normal in folate. Homocysteine ↑ in both.
  • Hypersegmented neutrophils ≥6 lobes; retic LOW, LDH high = ineffective erythropoiesis.
  • Fish tapeworm (raw lake fish + ribbons) steals B12. Hookworm = iron.
  • Oral B12 works by ~1% PASSIVE DIFFUSION of a 1,000 µg dose.
  • Lead blocks ALA dehydratase → ALA ↑, PBG NORMAL. AIP blocks below → BOTH ↑.
3 · ANEMIA II

Hemolysis

  • Shared: retics ↑, LDH ↑, unconjugated bilirubin ↑.
  • Intravascular ONLY: pink plasma, hemoglobinuria, ABSENT haptoglobin, URINE HEMOSIDERIN. Extravascular only: spherocytes, splenomegaly.
  • Corrected retics = retic % × (hematocrit ÷ 45). Above 2–3% = marrow responding.
  • MCHC >36–37 = spherocytes or artefact. Retics are BIGGER with LESS hemoglobin — they lower it.
  • Ball → HS · Bite → G6PD · Bull's-eye → thal · Crescent → sickle · Fragment → microangiopathy · Howell-Jolly → no spleen.
  • HS: Coombs NEGATIVE. G6PD: assay falsely normal during crisis.
  • Sickle: aplastic crisis = retic LOW (parvovirus). Fever = emergency.
4 · TRANSFUSION

Products & reactions

  • Transfuse below 7 g/dL, one unit then reassess (TRICC).
  • Platelets: room temp → most common infectious risk. Not for ITP/TTP/dengue.
  • 30% factor activity is enough to clot. Warfarin reversal = 4F-PCC + vitamin K.
  • ABO antibodies = natural IgM → complement → intravascular. Alloantibodies = IgG → extravascular.
  • Direct Coombs = on the cell. Indirect = in the plasma.
  • TRALI hypotensive, normal neck veins, diuresis no help. TACO hypertensive, raised veins, diuresis helps.
  • Acute hemolytic = wrong blood in tube, a "never event". GVHD → prevent by irradiation.
5 · HEMOSTASIS PHARM

Charge · interactions · reversal

  • UFH ~15,000 Da, strongly NEGATIVE → Xa + IIa → protamine (a POSITIVE base) FULL. LMWH ~5,000 → mostly Xa → PARTIAL ~60%. Fondaparinux ~1,500 → Xa only → NONE.
  • Fondaparinux doesn't bind PF4 → can't cause HIT → treats it.
  • HIT: platelets fall d5–14, >50%, danger is THROMBOSIS. Argatroban/bivalirudin/fondaparinux. Never warfarin alone (protein C → gangrene).
  • Warfarin "1972" = factors 2,7,9,10 + proteins C&S. ↑INR: amiodarone, metronidazole, TMP-SMX, fluconazole, cipro, acute alcohol. ↓INR: rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's wort, chronic alcohol, greens. Reversal = vitamin K + 4F-PCC, BOTH.
  • Dabigatran → idarucizumab. Xabans → 4F-PCC (andexanet withdrawn 2026). Argatroban/bivalirudin → none.
  • Apixaban safest · Rivaroxaban needs a meal · Edoxaban avoid if CrCl >95.
  • Aspirin irreversible 7–10 d · clopidogrel prodrug CYP2C19 (not omeprazole) · prasugrel prohibited after stroke · ticagrelor dyspnea + aspirin ≤100 mg · cangrelor IV minutes.
  • Thrombolytic bleed → cryoprecipitate + tranexamic acid.
  • White clot (arterial) = antiplatelet. Red clot (venous) = anticoagulant.
6 · ANEMIA PHARM

Iron, vitamins, ESAs

  • Iron absorbed as Fe²⁺, needs acid. Blocked by PPIs, antacids, calcium, tea. Chelates tetracyclines & quinolones.
  • Acute iron toxicity → deferoxamine. Chronic overload → deferasirox / deferiprone. Every chelator has "fe".
  • IV iron: premedicate with methylprednisolone, NOT diphenhydramine.
  • Folinic acid (leucovorin) rescues methotrexate — already past DHFR. Folic acid is useless there.
  • ESAs: all boxed warnings above hemoglobin 11. Cut dose if rise >1 g/dL in 2 weeks. Replete iron first.
  • Vadadustat = HIF-PHI, oral, "fakes altitude" → hypertension + clots.
  • Hydroxyurea raises HbF (≥20% helps); boxed myelosuppression + malignancy.
7 · INFECTION

Sepsis · HIV · parasites · VHF

  • Sepsis = dysregulated response; cultures negative 30–50%. qSOFA: RR ≥22, SBP ≤100, altered mentation. Procalcitonin = bacterial.
  • HIV: p24 easiest to detect; eclipse d0–10 detects nothing. CD4 <200 defines AIDS; <50 = highest OI risk. NNRTI — one mutation kills the class.
  • vivax & Ovale make hypnOzoites → relapse. Schizonts are the AWAKE stage, present in every species.
  • Smear trigger: fever + travel + thrombocytopenia. Falciparum infects all ages → >10%; irregular fever; most dangerous.
  • Babesia: Ixodes NYMPH, no tick history expected; azithromycin + atovaquone (NOT doxycycline).
  • Bartonella inoculum = flea FECES. Plague = bipolar "safety pin" + bubo.
  • VHF: acetaminophen NEVER NSAIDs. Hantavirus incubates 3–9 WEEKS; Lassa → deafness + ribavirin; CCHF → Hyalomma tick.
8 · LABORATORY

Measured, calculated, and lying

  • Measured: red cell count, MCV (impedance), hemoglobin (photometry 555 nm). Calculated: hematocrit, MCH, MCHC, RDW.
  • Lipemia / high bilirubin / huge WBC → hemoglobin falsely HIGH (optical). Hematocrit is particle-counted, so it stays honest.
  • Side scatter = GRANULARITY → eosinophil highest. Forward = size → monocyte biggest. Lymphocyte bottom-left.
  • Normal MCV + very high RDW = TWO populations (post-transfusion, or microcytic + reticulocytosis).
  • Cold agglutinins: RBC count ↓, MCV ↑, MCHC ↑↑ — look for the whole package.
  • Read the monolayer; the feather edge makes fake spherocytes.
9 · BENIGN WHITE CELL

Counts & immunodeficiency

  • ANC <1,000 risk · <500 own flora · <200 fever may be the only sign.
  • Reactive: lower N:C, coarse chromatin, varied, other lines normal. Malignant: high N:C, open chromatin, prominent nucleoli, monoclonal, cytopenias.
  • Auer rod = malignant. Nucleolus alone is not. Basophilia → CML (low leukocyte alkaline phosphatase; leukemoid is high).
  • CGD → NADPH oxidase, catalase-positive organisms. Bruton → BTK, absent tonsils, well till 6 months. DiGeorge → 22q11, irradiated products, no live vaccines. SCID → sick from month 2, TREC screen.
10 · COAGULATION I

Bleeding

  • Mucocutaneous → primary (VWD/platelet). Joint + muscle → hemophilia. Delayed → factor 13.
  • Intrinsic 12-11-9-8 = aPTT · extrinsic 7 = PT · common 10-5-2-fibrinogen = both. Liver makes all but VWF and factor 8.
  • 50% rule; mix CORRECTS = deficiency, FAILS = inhibitor.
  • VWD: activity ≈ antigen → quantitative; activity ≪ antigen → type 2.
  • Bernard–Soulier = GPIb (ristocetin low, big platelets). Glanzmann = GPIIb/IIIa (all but ristocetin low).
  • Factor 5 normal = vitamin K. Factor 5 low = liver.
  • DIC: both times ↑, fibrinogen falling, D-dimer ↑↑ → treat the cause.
11 · COAGULATION II

Clotting

  • All microangiopathies: PT, aPTT, fibrinogen NORMAL. DIC: abnormal.
  • TTP = ADAMTS13 → plasma exchange, NO platelets. HUS = Shiga toxin → kidney. aHUS = alternative complement → eculizumab.
  • Lose a brake (antithrombin, protein C/S) → RR 15–19×. Push accelerator (factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A) → RR 3–5×.
  • G20210A is a 3′ UTR mutation — normal protein, more of it.
  • Antiphospholipid: mix FAILS, extra phospholipid CORRECTS; persist 12 weeks; use warfarin, DOACs cause harm.
  • Duration: provoked 3 months · unprovoked ≥3 then decide · second unprovoked long-term · cancer = LMWH.
12 · LYMPH NODE

Normal & the mimics

  • Cortex = B · paracortex = T · medulla = plasma cells. Subcapsular sinus = where metastasis lands first.
  • Reactive center: polarized, tingible body macrophages, intact mantle, BCL2 NEGATIVE. t(14;18) turns it back on = follicular lymphoma.
  • Formalin kills flow cytometry and karyotype.
  • Mononucleosis RS-like cells RETAIN CD20/OCT2/PAX5; true RS cells have LOST them. CD30 and EBV are shared and useless.
  • Kikuchi: young Asian woman, necrosis with NO neutrophils, CD68+. Castleman: onion-skinning + lollipop vessel. Rosai–Dorfman: emperipolesis, S100+.
  • HLH: high ferritin, LOW fibrinogen, 5 of 8.
13 · MALIGNANCY

The construct & the diseases

  • Less differentiated = aggressive = CURABLE. Indolent = incurable. >20% blasts = acute.
  • APL t(15;17) → ATRA on suspicion. MDS = hypercellular + cytopenias. CML t(9;22). MPNs JAK2 → thrombosis.
  • CD5+ → CLL (CD23+) vs mantle (CD23−, t(11;14)). CD10+ → germinal center.
  • Follicular: BCL2+, t(14;18), paratrabecular. Burkitt: BCL2−, t(8;14), Ki-67 >95%, starry sky.
  • Hairy cell: dry tap + monocytopenia. MALT: lymphoepithelial lesions, treat H. pylori.
  • Hodgkin: contiguous spread, excisional biopsy, RS <10% of node. NLPHL = popcorn cells, CD20+.
  • Myeloma: damage from the PRODUCT. CRAB. MGUS 1%/yr, smoldering 10%/yr — neither treated.
14 · ANTINEOPLASTIC

Chemo Man & the targeted classes

  • Log kill = constant FRACTION. P-glycoprotein → cross-resistance.
  • Cyclophosphamide → cystitis (mesna) · bleomycin → lung, marrow SPARED · cisplatin → ear/kidney · doxorubicin → heart (cumulative cap) · vincristine → nerves, NEVER intrathecal.
  • Irinotecan EARLY diarrhea = cholinergic (acetylcholinesterase inhibition) with sweating, tearing, drooling, BRADYCARDIA → ATROPINE. LATE = loperamide. Steroids are for checkpoint colitis.
  • Size decides site: antibodies outside the cell, small molecules inside.
  • ADC: second word = payload. Checkpoint toxicity = anything "-itis" → steroids. CRS → tocilizumab; ICANS → dexamethasone.
  • Abiraterone blocks CYP17 → precursors shunt to the MINERALOCORTICOID pathway → hypokalemia + hypertension → co-prescribe PREDNISONE (suppresses the ACTH drive). Plus hepatotoxicity.
  • SERMs protect bone; aromatase inhibitors destroy it. GnRH agonist → flare.
15 · LYMPHATICS & VTE

Lymphatics

  • Primary function = return PROTEINS. Lymphatic walls are MORE permeable, so lymph is protein-heavy. Obstruction traps protein → it holds water osmotically → fibrosis. Venous obstruction = hydrostatic, low-protein transudate.
  • Never a LEFT internal jugular line — thoracic duct → chylothorax.
  • Peyer's patches → IgA. Ileocecal resection in an infant → translocation sepsis.
  • Lymphedema: square toes = Stemmer sign; unilateral + negative ultrasound. Developed world iatrogenic; worldwide filariasis.
  • DVT → Doppler first. Apixaban/Rivaroxaban Alone; Dabigatran/Edoxaban need Days of heparin.
16 · PEDS, SPLEEN & RX

The child and the spleen

  • ITP: isolated low platelets, megakaryocytes INCREASED, splenomegaly RARE. Acute child 80% resolve.
  • WELL child + no organomegaly + ACELLULAR marrow = aplastic. SICK child + splenomegaly + PACKED marrow = leukemia. Lymphadenopathy occurs in both. White count may be LOW in leukemia.
  • Transfuse 10–15 cc/kg (raises ~2–3 g/dL), target ~7 NOT normal — chronic anemia is compensated; fast correction → heart failure. Then iron + cut cow's milk.
  • Fanconi = absent THUMBS; TAR = thumbs PRESENT. Wiskott-Aldrich = SMALL platelets.
  • Portal hypertension = commonest hypersplenism; splenectomy NOT indicated. Splenic vein thrombosis → splenectomy IS curative.
  • Prescription: the commonest omission is the quantity. No trailing zero; always a leading zero; write "units" and "daily".
17 · TRAUMA & METASTASIS

Spleen, edema, spread

  • UNSTABLE → FAST → OR. Never CT an unstable patient. Kehr sign = left shoulder pain.
  • Delayed rupture day 5–6. Platelets >1 million post-op → aspirin.
  • Edema clock: hours = compartment/hematoma · days = DVT/hypoalbuminemia · weeks = lymphedema.
  • Compartment: pain on PASSIVE STRETCH; pulses stay palpable; ΔP <30 → fasciotomy.
  • Carcinoma spreads by lymphatics; sarcoma by blood. Virchow node = LEFT supraclavicular → abdominal primary.
  • Testis → para-aortic. Anal canal above dentate → internal iliac; below → superficial inguinal.
18 · GERIATRICS

Function predicts outcome

  • An ADL-disabled 75-year-old has the life expectancy of an independent 85-year-old.
  • 4Ms: What Matters · Medication · Mentation · Mobility (+ Multicomplexity).
  • Always: brief screen → longer instrument → intervention. Mini-Cog → MoCA/SLUMS. PHQ-2 → PHQ-9 (therapy BEFORE drugs). TUG/STEADI → physical therapy. Katz/Lawton–Brody → occupational therapy.
  • You do not administer every tool — choose by the complaint. Beers Criteria for medication harm.
  • Therapists predict successful discharge home; physicians and social workers do not.
  • Marrow cellularity = (100 − age)%. "Inflammaging" in treated HIV.
