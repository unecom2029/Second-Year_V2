SECTION 01

Antipsychotics & Movement Side Effects

9 missed · 2 flagged

Your single weakest area. Almost every miss here comes from one of two things: not placing a side effect on the EPS timeline (EPS = extrapyramidal symptoms, the movement side effects of dopamine blockade), or not ranking drugs on the side-effect tables. Both are memorization, not reasoning — which means they are entirely fixable.

✗ MISSED

Missed twice: acute dystonia (Q40) and tardive dyskinesia (Q139)
The rule — 4·4·4·4

Why you missed it

You reached for the anticholinergic reflex in both directions. For acute dystonia the drug was right but the route was the answer — oral is too slow when the jaw and voice are already involved and laryngospasm is the feared next step. Discriminator

Anticholinergics fix the first two rows only. Akathisia gets propranolol; tardive dyskinesia gets drug withdrawal plus a VMAT2 inhibitor — vesicular monoamine transporter 2, the pump that loads dopamine into vesicles. And tardive dyskinesia characteristically worsens transiently when the antipsychotic is stopped — that's the expected course, not treatment failure.

✗ MISSED

Dopamine-2 receptor occupancy: 70% works, 78–80% causes side effects

Q89
The rule

Therapeutic antipsychotic effect emerges at roughly 70% occupancy of striatal dopamine-2 (D2) receptors. Extrapyramidal symptoms begin at roughly 78–80%. That ten-point gap is the entire therapeutic window.

Why it matters clinically

This is the reason raising the dose is always wrong for akathisia. The side effect is occupancy-dependent, so escalating pushes you further above the EPS threshold and predictably makes the restlessness worse while buying no extra efficacy.

✗ MISSED

Smoking speeds up a liver enzyme — in both directions

Q68 — resumed smoking → relapse. Compare Q137 (stopped smoking → toxicity), which you got right.
The rule

The polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons in cigarette smoke — not the nicotine — induce CYP1A2, one of the cytochrome P450 liver enzymes that clears drugs. Olanzapine and clozapine are the two antipsychotics that enzyme handles.

Why you missed it

You got the version where smoking stops (level rises → sedation, seizure) but missed the mirror image where smoking resumes (level falls → relapse). Same mechanism, opposite arrow. Roughly 95% of patients with psychotic disorders smoke, so both directions are routine. (Induce = speed the enzyme up, so the drug is cleared faster and levels fall. Inhibit = slow it down, so levels rise.)

Discriminator

Induction → treatment failure. Inhibition → toxicity. Nicotine patches change nothing — they don't contain the hydrocarbons.

✗ MISSED

First-episode schizophrenia: second-generation antipsychotic first

Q76
The rule

The ladder is second-generation antipsychotic (SGA) → SGA or first-generation antipsychotic (FGA) → clozapine → clozapine augmentation. All five major guideline bodies agree on a second-generation agent first.

The nuance that may have tripped you

Two large head-to-head trials — CATIE and CUtLASS — found first-generation agents (perphenazine especially) perform about as well as second-generation ones, and cost less. That is a real finding — but guideline position and comparative-efficacy data are different questions, and an exam can ask either. Clozapine is never first-line; it needs two failed trials.

✗ MISSED

Psychosis in Parkinson disease → quetiapine

Q211
The rule

Quetiapine (memory hook: Suzie Q) is the antipsychotic of choice for psychosis in Parkinson disease and Lewy body dementia, because its dopamine-2 blockade is weak and loosely bound — controlling hallucinations with the least worsening of parkinsonism. Pimavanserin is the other named option.

Read for this first

⚑ FLAGGED

Risperidone → hyperprolactinemia

Q141 — you got this right, but flagged it
The rule

Dopamine tonically inhibits prolactin via the tuberoinfundibular tract. Block dopamine-2 receptors there → prolactin disinhibited → galactorrhea (milk production), amenorrhea (periods stop) and gynecomastia (breast tissue growth in men). Risperidone and its metabolite paliperidone top the prolactin table.

The three rule-outs the stem will clear

Negative pregnancy test, normal thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), full visual fields (excluding a chiasm-compressing prolactinoma). And watch the quiet detail: an unchanged body mass index steers you away from the “-pines,” whose famous effect is weight gain.

SECTION 02

Substance & Toxicology

5 missed · 5 flagged

You flagged as many here as you missed — this is a low-confidence area even where you're getting things right. Most of it reduces to two organizing rules and a handful of antidote pairings.

✗ MISSED

Uppers in, downers out — when psychosis appears

Q122
The rule

Stimulants and hallucinogens cause psychosis during intoxication. Alcohol, benzodiazepines and barbiturates cause psychosis during withdrawal.

Apply it in both directions

Amphetamine withdrawal does not cause psychosis (its absence supports the diagnosis). Alcohol intoxication generally doesn't either — alcoholic hallucinosis appears at 24–48 h off, and DTs peak around day 2.

✗ MISSED

Varenicline — partial agonist doing two jobs

Q75
The rule

Varenicline is a partial agonist at the α4β2 (alpha-4-beta-2) nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. Partial agonism does double duty: enough stimulation to blunt craving and withdrawal, while occupying the receptor so that smoked nicotine delivers less reward. Most common adverse effect: nausea.

Contrast

Bupropion (sold as Zyban for this purpose) works through dopamine/norepinephrine reuptake inhibition and its limitation is lowering the seizure threshold. Nicotine replacement is the third, step-down approach.

✗ MISSED

Naloxone wears off before the opioid does

Q117
The rule

Naloxone's half-life is shorter than most opioids. Re-sedation as it wears off — renarcotization — is expected, not surprising. Redose and observe; a continuous infusion for recurrence.

Two related facts

Titrate to respiratory drive, not to consciousness — overshooting precipitates acute withdrawal. Rapid intravenous push risks arrhythmia and rare flash pulmonary edema from the catecholamine surge. And an extended-release opioid outlasts naloxone by far, so discharge is never the answer.

✗ MISSED

The LOT benzodiazepines in liver disease

Q101
The rule

Most benzodiazepines undergo liver phase I oxidation into long-lived active metabolites that accumulate in cirrhosis. Three skip it and go straight to phase II glucuronidation (a simpler, better-preserved liver step):

Lorazepam · Oxazepam · Temazepam

Same reasoning, three settings

Liver impairment · older adults (increased body fat expands the volume of distribution for these lipophilic drugs) · alcohol withdrawal in a patient with cirrhosis. Oxazepam is the classic detox pick; lorazepam when you also want an intermediate half-life.

✗ MISSED

New-onset hypersexuality → look for a cause

Q204
The rule

New hypersexuality is a red flag demanding a search for an underlying cause, never a primary psychiatric or paraphilic diagnosis. The named culprits: dopamine agonists, brain injury, Klüver–Bucy syndrome, Kleine–Levin syndrome.

Discriminator

Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole — and partial agonists like aripiprazole) carry a warning for an impulse-control cluster: hypersexuality, pathological gambling, compulsive shopping, binge eating. Look for a dose increase preceding the behavior change, and for a lifelong baseline that contradicts it.

⚑ FLAGGED

Tricyclic overdose — the ECG is the diagnosis

Q57
The rule

The three C's — Coma, Convulsions, Cardiotoxicity — layered on a full anticholinergic toxidrome (hot, dry, flushed, mydriasis, ileus, retention).

By its indication pair. Neuropathic pain + depression, or migraine prophylaxis + depression, means amitriptyline/nortriptyline. Tricyclics carry roughly a one-week lethal supply, which is why they're avoided at meaningful suicide risk.

⚑ FLAGGED

Z-drugs → complex sleep behaviors

Q79
The rule

Zolpidem, eszopiclone, zaleplon act selectively at the BZ1 subtype of the GABA-A receptor (gamma-aminobutyric acid type A — the brain's main inhibitory, or calming, receptor) and are indicated for sleep only. Signature adverse effect: parasomnias — sleep-eating, sleep-sex, and notoriously sleep-driving, with anterograde amnesia for the episodes.

Two paired facts

Because they act at the benzodiazepine site, flumazenil reverses them in overdose. Any occurrence of a complex sleep behavior means the drug is stopped permanently (boxed warning).

⚑ FLAGGED

MDMA → hyponatremia (not just serotonin)

Q132
The rule

MDMA (ecstasy) reverses the serotonin transporter — hyperthermia, diaphoresis, mydriasis, bruxism, clonus. Separately and testably, it drives SIADH — syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone, where the body holds on to water it should excrete; combined with heavy free-water intake this produces hyponatremia → cerebral edema → seizure.

The clue reads as sensible behavior

“She kept refilling her water bottle” looks like responsible hydration and is the mechanism. Serotonin excess explains the fever and clonus; it does not explain a seizure — the sodium does.

⚑ FLAGGED

CAGE — two is positive, and a screen is not a diagnosis

Q83
The rule

Cut down · Annoyed · Guilty · Eye-opener. Any single yes flags risk; ≥2 is a positive screen warranting fuller assessment against DSM-5 criteria. The other named instrument is AUDIT-C — the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test, short form, scored 0–12.

Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome — FINISH

Q169 — both missed and flagged
The rule

Flu-like · Insomnia · Nausea · Imbalance · Sensory disturbance (the “brain zaps”) · Hyperarousal. Management: restart the antidepressant and taper slowly.

Risk tracks inversely with half-life

Paroxetine is the worst offender (shortest half-life, no active metabolite), then venlafaxine and fluvoxamine. Fluoxetine (2–4 day half-life + active metabolite) essentially never does it. ✗ MISSED

Duloxetine: the perfect-looking answer with a liver problem

Q158
The rule

Mnemonic: DULL-oxetine — it dulls pain but punishes the liver and the eye. Contraindicated in liver impairment (associated with fatal hepatotoxicity) and in uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma.

The trap structure — learn to recognize it

Duloxetine carries indications for depression, neuropathic pain and fibromyalgia, so it is the obvious fit in any stem pairing depression with pain. That is exactly why it's the trap. The stem then buries a contraindication in the past medical history and clears the other one explicitly, so only one clue decides it.

Watch for reversed polarity

These stems usually ask which drug to avoid, not which to choose. Read the direction before scanning the options.

✗ MISSED

Augmenting a maxed-out SSRI in a patient with a substance history

Q99
The rule

Two constraints converge: already at maximum dose (so no escalation) and substance use disorder in remission (so no benzodiazepine, ever — cross-dependence, meaning one drug can substitute for and sustain dependence on the other). Buspirone is a listed generalized anxiety disorder option both as monotherapy and as add-on to a baseline antidepressant.

General principle

A partial response to a maximally dosed antidepressant calls for augmentation, not abandonment of an agent that's clearly working.

✗ MISSED

Buspirone feels like nothing — and that's the mechanism

Q94
The rule

Buspirone is a 5-HT1A partial agonist (a serotonin receptor subtype) with no activity at GABA-A, the receptor benzodiazepines act on — which is precisely why it causes no sedation, no euphoria, no dependence, and delivers nothing acutely. It needs 4–6 weeks, like an antidepressant.

The clinical lesson buried in it

Patients coming off a benzodiazepine say it is “like taking water,” because they're comparing against a drug that worked in 30 minutes. Set that expectation before the switch or they'll stop it before it can work.

⚑ FLAGGED

Bulimia + depression → fluoxetine

Q62
The rule

Fluoxetine is the only agent carrying an FDA indication for bulimia nervosa alongside major depressive disorder — one drug treats both. Bupropion is contraindicated in eating disorders regardless of seizure history.

Read the acid–base panel to identify the purging method

Vomiting → hypochloremic, hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis (low chloride, low potassium, high bicarbonate). Laxatives → metabolic acidosis with low sodium and high chloride. Hypokalemia is common to both.

✗ MISSED

Valproate + pregnancy planning happens before conception

Q22
The rule

The neural tube closes very early — often before a woman knows she is pregnant. So valproate planning must happen preconception, not at the first positive test: transition off valproate, and supplement high-dose folic acid (above what a standard over-the-counter prenatal vitamin provides).

Why antipsychotics are often preferred in pregnancy

Not because they're proven safe — because most simply lack pregnancy data, and that data gap is judged preferable to valproate's and carbamazepine's documented teratogenicity. Clozapine is the antipsychotic with real accumulated outcome data.

⚑ FLAGGED

Lithium in pregnancy — trimester-specific, not uniform

Q23
The rule

Ebstein anomaly (right atrial enlargement + downwardly displaced tricuspid valve) arises from disrupted cardiac organogenesis — so risk is concentrated in the first trimester. Many clinicians minimize or hold lithium in the first trimester and resume or increase it later, once cardiac formation is complete.

The four exceptions to “lithium is the default”

Pregnancy · significant renal impairment · cardiac conduction disease · difficult-to-manage thyroid disease. This is also why a serum pregnancy test (hCG) is part of lithium prescreening, alongside a complete blood count, creatinine and blood urea nitrogen (kidney function), thyroid function tests, and an electrocardiogram.

⚑ FLAGGED

Carbamazepine defeats oral contraceptives

Q28
The rule

Carbamazepine is a potent inducer of hepatic metabolism — it even autoinduces its own clearance, which is why levels drift downward over the first weeks on an unchanged dose. That induction makes standard oral contraceptive pills unreliable → recommend a non-hormonal method such as an intrauterine device (IUD), especially since carbamazepine is itself teratogenic.

Carbamazepine's four signature liabilities

Agranulocytosis · hyponatremia (low sodium, from SIADH — the body retaining water inappropriately) · autoinduction · drug interactions generally. It's described as a genuinely “dirty” drug.

⚑ FLAGGED

Olanzapine–fluoxetine: the only approved antidepressant combination in bipolar

Q210
The rule

Fluoxetine + olanzapine is the only approved antidepressant-containing combination in bipolar disorder, and the indication is maintenance-phase — specifically not acute mania.

Why the pairing exists

Antidepressants in bipolar disorder can worsen depression or precipitate a manic switch, so they are never used as unopposed monotherapy — which is exactly why the approved product bundles an antipsychotic with the antidepressant. Any antidepressant use in bipolar sits on top of a mood stabilizer.

Legal, Ethics & Forensics

✗ MISSED

Tarasoff: named victim + credible plan

Q19
The threshold

A specific, identifiable victim facing imminent and probable danger. The test is named target + credible plan — who, how, and when — not violent content.

What does NOT meet the bar

Fantasies and rhetorical anger, when clinical judgment holds the patient doesn't intend to act (“I could kill my brother, I just hate him”). A confessed past crime stays confidential. Only prospective risk to an identifiable person breaks the seal.

When it does fire

“Warn” in practice means contact the police and let them notify and protect. Tarasoff II broadens duty-to-warn into duty-to-protect, satisfied by notifying law enforcement and documenting it. Genuinely borderline? Get a second clinician's opinion before deciding not to report.

✗ MISSED

Do NOT use single-session debriefing after trauma

Q196
The rule

Acute stress disorder is treated with immediate, ongoing individual therapy and counseling — but specifically not mandatory one-time group debriefing, which has not been shown to prevent PTSD and may worsen outcomes by compelling re-exposure without follow-up support.

Don't over-correct

✗ MISSED

Seasonal pattern vs. atypical features — the calendar, not the symptoms

Q60
The rule

Seasonal pattern is defined temporally: episodes begin and remit at characteristic times of year in a regular relationship, with no episodes outside that window. Atypical features are defined symptomatically, starting with mood reactivity.

Why they're confusable

Winter episodes very often carry atypical symptoms — hypersomnia, hyperphagia — so the symptom lists overlap heavily. The calendar is the only clean discriminator, which is why vignettes plant an off-cycle episode (e.g. a mid-July onset) to rule seasonal pattern out.

Atypical features — the required one first

Mood reactivity is mandatory. Then the “gains”: hypersomnia, hyperphagia with weight gain, leaden paralysis, and longstanding rejection sensitivity present outside episodes. Atypical is the most common depressive subtype, despite the name.

✗ MISSED

Benzodiazepines in pregnancy — the 2–8 week window

Postoperative delirium — check the anticholinergic burden

Q33
The rule

Delirium = acute onset from a documented baseline + fluctuating attention with lucid intervals + visual hallucinations. The rule-out ladder puts substances and MEDICATIONS ahead of any primary psychiatric or degenerative cause.

The stacked order set

Oxybutynin + diphenhydramine + cyclobenzaprine — three anticholinergics in one post-op order set. Anticholinergic burden is a leading reversible cause in older surgical patients.

The recurring offender

Diphenhydramine is ordered as a sleep aid on exactly the patients least able to tolerate it. It's the same reason an antihistamine is the wrong choice for agitation in delirium.

Delusional disorder and its six subtypes

Q82 (erotomanic)
The rule

A fixed, non-bizarre (plausible) delusion for ≥1 month, with preserved functioning and no other prominent psychotic features. The subtype is assigned by content.

Persecutory · Somatic · Erotomanic · Grandiose · Jealous · Mixed

The test for “non-bizarre”

Could the belief in principle be true? A news anchor secretly loving a viewer could in principle be true, even though the evidence says otherwise.
