⭐ One-Page High-Yield Summary

Introduction to Therapy — condensed for last-minute review. Full explanations and examples are in the sections above.

Conditioning

Classical: innate response + neutral stimulus → response transfers to the neutral stimulus (Pavlov, Little Albert). Usually involuntary.

Operant: reward/punishment shapes a voluntary behavior (Skinner).

Reinforcement↑ behavior (add/remove stimulus)
Punishment↓ behavior (add/remove stimulus)
Extinctionbehavior fades, reinforcement stops
Shapingreward successive approximations
Modelinglearning by observation
Systematic desensitizationgraded exposure ↓ fear (habituation)
Aversive conditioningnoxious stimulus + unwanted behavior (disulfiram+EtOH)

Skinner's Quadrants

↑ behavior↓ behavior
Add stimulusPositive reinforcementPositive punishment
Remove stimulusNegative reinforcementNegative punishment

Trigger test: reinforcer = creature did what you wanted; punishment = creature did what you didn't want.

Freud's Structural Model

Id — raw drives (sexual/aggressive), unfiltered.

Ego — basic reality-testing restraint.

Superego — fine-tuned social/moral restraint.

Transference: patient projects past feelings onto provider.

Countertransference: provider projects past feelings onto patient.

🧠 Mnemonics

  • SASH = mature defenses: Sublimation, Altruism, Suppression, Humor
  • "Borders split countries" — Splitting → Borderline PD
  • Repression = unconscious; Suppression = conscious & deliberate
  • Fixation = stuck at a stage; Regression = slide back to one
  • DBT → BPD (Marsha Linehan, 4 modules)
  • Clusters: A = Weird, B = Wild, C = Worried

Ego Defenses — Quick Definitions

  • Denial — refuses painful reality
  • Projection — attributes own impulse to others
  • Reaction formation — opposite attitude masks true feeling
  • Splitting ⭐ — all-good/all-bad; → BPD
  • Displacement — redirects emotion to a safer target
  • Rationalization — logical excuse to avoid self-blame
  • Regression — reverts to an earlier stage under stress
  • Identification — unconsciously mimics someone powerful
  • Intellectualization — facts/logic to avoid emotion
  • Acting out — behavior instead of acknowledging feeling
  • Undoing — symbolic act "reverses" a past deed
  • Dissociation — detachment from self/memory under stress
  • Idealization — sees another as more perfect than real
  • Fixation — stuck at an earlier developmental stage
  • Isolation of affect — fact without its feeling
  • Passive aggression — indirect hostility, no confrontation
  • Repression — unconscious, involuntary blocking
  • Sublimation (mature) — impulse → productive outlet
  • Altruism (mature) — distress managed via helping others
  • Suppression (mature) — conscious, deliberate, temporary
  • Humor (mature) — comedy deflects discomfort

Efficacy Highlights

1977 meta-analysis: effect size 0.8 → therapy beat ~79–80% of untreated controls.

TDCRP: strong alliance + placebo > weak alliance + real medication.

Common factors (Frank/Goldfried/Castonguay): confiding relationship, corrective experience, insight, mastery — matter more than the specific brand of therapy.
